### 1. DocuSign eSignature (templates, anchor tagging, routing, bulk send) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** DocuSign is the default way agreements get executed — envelopes with routing order and conditional recipient routing, reusable templates and composite templates, anchor-text field tagging, bulk send off a list, signer authentication (SMS / KBA / ID Verify), Connect webhooks for status, and Maestro for multi-step agreement workflows. The REST API v2.1 exposes essentially everything the web UI does, including the tab (field) geometry.

**Why people bounce off it:** Building a reusable template. To make one you must drag tabs onto a PDF pixel-by-pixel, understand *anchor text* tagging versus absolute x/y positioning, distinguish recipient **roles** from actual recipients, set routing order so counter-signers fire in sequence, and then confront the conditional-routing rule editor. The observable result is that most teams never make templates at all — they re-drag signature fields on every single send, forever — and conditional routing goes untouched even when the business obviously needs it. Bulk send is a second cliff: it exists, it's exactly what the ops person wants, and the CSV format plus recipient-role mapping stops them.

**AI operator:** An agreement operator that reads the PDF itself, infers who signs what and where (signature, initials, date, text, checkbox), and emits an anchor-tagged template rather than a coordinate-tagged one — so the template survives the document being re-flowed next quarter. Preloaded skills: template authoring from a document, recipient roles with routing order and conditions, bulk send from a spreadsheet, and in-flight envelope babysitting (reminders, corrections, voids) with plain-English status narration. Every send is preceded by a rendered preview of the tag placement and the routing graph.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Take this 14-page MSA, put an initial box on every page for the customer, have Legal counter-sign only after both customer signers finish, and send it to the 30 vendors in this CSV."* → The AI creates a template with anchor tabs (`/init/` on each page, `/sig1/`, `/date1/`), defines three roles at routingOrder 1, 1, 2, builds a bulkSendList from the CSV, fires the batch, subscribes to Connect events, and auto-reminds anyone idle at day 3 — reporting back "27 complete, 2 viewed-not-signed, 1 bounced email."

**Integration path:** DocuSign eSignature REST API v2.1 — Envelopes, Templates, Recipients, Tabs (including anchor tabs), BulkSend, Connect webhooks — plus the Admin API for users/SSO and the Maestro workflow API. Auth is JWT Grant for service integrations (one-time app consent) or Auth Code for user-context. Gotchas: the mandatory demo → production go-live review before you can touch a real account, hourly API request caps that vary by plan, and envelope-allowance accounting on every create (so a runaway agent costs real money, not just quota).

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 2. Xero (bank reconciliation, coding, tracking categories) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Real double-entry general ledger with bank feeds, multi-currency and FX revaluation, tracking categories, repeating invoices, GST/VAT return prep and regional payroll — deep enough that accounting practices run entire client books on it. 4M+ subscribers, dominant in AU/NZ/UK and growing elsewhere.

**Why people bounce off it:** The **bank reconciliation "Find & Match" screen**. It is the one place a non-accountant sits every week, and the moment a payout doesn't match a single invoice one-to-one — a Stripe payout covering 40 orders minus fees, a part-payment, a bank fee split — they give up and code it to Suspense. Behind that sit the chart of accounts and tax-rate setup, tracking categories versus contact groups (people pick the wrong one and can never report the way they wanted), conversion balances at migration, and realised versus unrealised FX. The pattern is universal: a small business owner does six months of "I'll fix it later" and then pays an accountant to unpick it.

**AI operator:** Talk-to-your-ledger. Preloaded skills for recoding mis-posted transactions, matching aggregate payouts to bank lines by reconstructing the underlying gross/fee split, raising and voiding invoices, issuing credit notes, creating tracking categories, and running/explaining reports in business English. Every batch is a diff-then-apply: it shows a per-line before/after table and the resulting P&L delta before it writes a single journal.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Every Stripe payout for last quarter got coded to Suspense — split them into Sales and Merchant Fees and reconcile them."* → The AI pulls `BankTransactions` for that account, fetches the matching Stripe payout and balance-transaction data, computes gross and fee per payout, posts corrected line items against the right accounts and tax rates, marks the bank lines reconciled — and first shows a 63-row before/after table plus "this moves £4,182 out of Suspense and adds £311 of deductible merchant fees."

**Integration path:** Xero Accounting API (REST, OAuth 2.0 + PKCE) with full write access on Invoices, BankTransactions, Journals, Contacts, Accounts and TrackingCategories, plus separate Payroll (AU/UK/NZ), Files, Projects and Assets APIs. Gotchas that shape the product: rate limits are tight — 60 calls/min/tenant, 5,000/day/tenant, 10,000/min app-wide — and **uncertified apps are capped at 25 connected organisations**, so scaling past a pilot requires passing Xero app certification. Bank *rules* have no public endpoint and bank *feeds* are partner-gated, so those two surfaces stay browser-driven; scope the v1 to coding, matching and reporting, which is where the pain actually is.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 3. Square / Square for Restaurants (Catalog, modifiers, multi-location) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Square runs the front counter for millions of small merchants. The Catalog is genuinely expressive — items with variations, item options, modifier lists with min/max selection rules and per-option pricing, categories, per-location price overrides, inclusive/exclusive taxes, discounts — plus Orders, Team/Labor with shifts and wage rates, and Inventory counts.

**Why people bounce off it:** The **modifier-set model in the menu editor**. Managers cannot keep straight the difference between *item options* (size/colour, which generate variations) and *modifier lists* (add-ons, which don't), and they have no idea why changing a price at one location changed it at the other. So the seasonal menu change across three locations becomes an evening of tapping through items one at a time, and 86'ing an item means walking to each till. This is a genuinely non-technical user — a café owner or a shift lead — doing bulk data work by hand.

**AI operator:** A menu-ops assistant that owns the Catalog in batch: bulk price moves scoped to a location, modifier-set surgery across a category, 86'ing with a scheduled restore, plus labour-side shift edits. It always renders the list of affected SKUs and the price delta before committing, because the merchant is standing at a counter and cannot audit a JSON diff.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Raise every burger 75 cents at the downtown location only, make bacon a $2 add-on on all of them, and 86 the lobster roll everywhere until Friday."* → The AI searches the catalog for the burger category, batch-upserts variation-level price overrides scoped to the downtown location ID, creates and attaches a $2.00 bacon modifier to each burger item, and marks the lobster roll unavailable at all locations with a Friday restore — all in one `BatchUpsertCatalogObjects` after showing the 14 affected SKUs.

**Integration path:** Square API — Catalog (`BatchUpsertCatalogObjects`, `UpdateItemModifierLists`, item options), Orders, Labor, Team, Inventory. OAuth 2.0 with per-seller tokens and granular scopes (`ITEMS_READ`/`ITEMS_WRITE`, `EMPLOYEES_*`, `TIMECARDS_*`); developer account and sandbox are free and instant — **no partner approval, no gatekeeper**, which is rare in this vertical. You never touch card data, so PCI stays Square's problem. Toast is the larger restaurant prize but requires a formal Partner Integration application plus certification before you get credentials — treat it as phase two, not the wedge.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 4. Google Search Console (Performance report + indexing diagnostics) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** GSC is the only ground truth for organic search — every query, page, country and device that surfaced a site in Google, plus indexing diagnostics, sitemap submission, live URL inspection, Core Web Vitals and rich-result validation. Free, and installed on essentially every site that cares about traffic.

**Why people bounce off it:** The Performance report is deliberately crippled in the UI: a 1,000-row export cap, filters that must be re-applied per dimension, and **no cross-dimension pivoting** — you cannot see query × page × country in one view, which is exactly the view every SEO question needs. The 16-month window has no cohorting. Then the Page Indexing report answers in folklore: "Crawled – currently not indexed", "Discovered – currently not indexed", "Duplicate without user-selected canonical" — strings that a small-business owner or a generalist marketer cannot act on and that even agencies argue about.

**AI operator:** An organic-search analyst with skills for multi-dimension `searchanalytics.query` with pagination well past the UI's 1,000 rows, striking-distance analysis (position 8–20 with real impressions), cannibalisation detection (two or more URLs competing on one query), CTR-versus-position outliers, before/after comparison anchored on a deploy date, sitemap submission, and bulk URL Inspection that translates each indexing status into a specific fix.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Which pages lost the most clicks after our June redesign, and why?"* → The AI pulls both 28-day windows grouped by page + query, diffs and ranks the losses, runs URL Inspection on the top 20 losers, and reports: "six dropped because the canonical now points at /shop; four lost position on 'X' to a competitor who added FAQ schema; three are 'Discovered – currently not indexed' because they're only linked from a JS-rendered carousel — here are the sitemaps to resubmit and the internal links to add."

**Integration path:** Google Search Console API v1 (`searchanalytics.query`, `sites`, `sitemaps`) plus the URL Inspection API (`urlInspection.index.inspect`). Free, no partner or app review to call it. Quotas are the real design constraint: ~1,200 QPM per site and per user for Search Analytics, and URL Inspection is hard-capped at **2,000 queries/day and 600 QPM per property** — a genuine ceiling on large sites, so the operator must sample intelligently rather than crawl. `webmasters.readonly` was reclassified non-sensitive in 2024; the read-write `webmasters` scope is still sensitive, so a publicly distributed app needs OAuth consent-screen verification.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 5. Canvas LMS (gradebook weighting, modules, differentiated dates) `OSS (AGPL)`

**Why it's powerful:** Canvas is the LMS for a very large share of North American K-12 and higher ed, and the API is enormous — 190+ resource groups covering modules with prerequisites and completion requirements, weighted assignment groups with drop rules, rubrics aligned to outcomes, differentiated due dates, SpeedGrader, quiz item banks, section-level overrides and LTI. It can genuinely model any course policy a teacher can articulate.

**Why people bounce off it:** Expressing an ordinary grading policy requires four unrelated screens. "Drop the lowest two quizzes, make quizzes 30%, and don't unlock Module 4 until they score 80% on the Unit 3 test" touches Assignment Groups (weights and drop rules), each assignment's own settings, Module requirements and prerequisites, and the Grade Posting Policy — and nothing in the UI tells you the four are related. Rubric setup in SpeedGrader and the "Assign To" differentiated-dates dialog are the other two places teachers quit. The user here is a teacher with 40 minutes of prep time, not an admin.

**AI operator:** A chat layer preloaded with the Canvas object model (courses, assignment_groups, assignments, modules, module_items, rubrics, overrides, submissions, gradebook history) that plans the whole multi-call transaction, shows a diff-style preview of every write in teacher language, then executes with rollback. Critically it should also *refuse coherently*: tell the teacher when the policy they described is mathematically impossible under the weighting scheme they've chosen.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Drop the lowest two quizzes, make quizzes 30% of the grade, and don't let anyone open Module 4 until they've scored 80% on the Unit 3 test — except Maya, she gets an extra week on everything."* → The AI creates/updates the Quizzes assignment group with `drop_lowest: 2` and a 30% weight, flips the course to weighted grading, sets a `min_score` completion requirement on the Unit 3 test module item plus a prerequisite on Module 4, then creates a per-student override for Maya on every assignment in the module.

**Integration path:** Canvas REST API v1. Two auth routes: a self-serve personal access token from Account → Settings (which root admins can disable), or OAuth2 with a Developer Key — and on Canvas Cloud those must be created and scoped by the institution's root admin, with 1-hour tokens plus refresh. Self-hosted Canvas has no gating at all. FERPA applies since you're touching grades and student identifiers: you need a DPA per institution and a no-training-on-customer-data commitment. Headwind worth pricing in: the April–May 2026 Instructure breach has institutional IT tightening third-party Canvas access — which lengthens the enterprise sale but strengthens the case for a teacher-token, zero-retention design.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 6. Stripe (Billing, subscription schedules, Tax, Connect) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** One stack covering card acquiring, subscriptions with usage-based metering, automatic multi-jurisdiction tax calculation and registration tracking, ML fraud scoring with a custom rule language, and full marketplace payouts with fee splits. Millions of businesses depend on it, and the API is arguably the best-documented commercial API in existence.

**Why people bounce off it:** **Subscription schedules and proration semantics.** Nobody — including the engineers — can predict what a plan change will actually invoice. `proration_behavior`, phase boundaries, backdating, credit balance application: the standard workflow is "make the change, see what invoices, issue a credit note to apologise." Behind that sit the Radar rules editor (a custom DSL you write in a Dashboard textarea with no test harness), Stripe Tax registrations and nexus thresholds, and Connect's `controller` properties × capabilities × payout schedule matrix. The person on the hook is usually a finance or RevOps person, not the engineer who did the original integration.

**AI operator:** A billing-change operator with a hard rule: nothing touches live mode until it has been replayed against a **test clock** in a Sandbox. Skills for pricing migrations, proration reasoning with an invoice-preview diff per customer, tax registration and nexus response, and Connect account configuration. The differentiator is the preview — "here is the exact invoice each of your 412 customers will receive" — which no dashboard gives you today.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Move everyone on the old annual plan to the new $49 price at their next renewal, don't prorate, and grandfather anyone who signed before June."* → The AI creates the new Price, builds `subscription_schedules` with a phase boundary at each customer's own renewal date, sets `proration_behavior=none`, excludes pre-June signups, runs the entire cohort against a test clock, prints the projected invoice delta per customer, and only then executes with idempotency keys.

**Integration path:** Stripe REST API (OAuth for platforms, restricted API keys, idempotency keys), Stripe CLI (`listen`, `trigger`, `fixtures`), Sandboxes, and `/v1/test_helpers/test_clocks` for time-travel validation — that last one is what makes this idea safe enough to sell. Honest gap: **Radar rules have no public CRUD API** — only `/v1/radar/value_lists` and `/v1/radar/value_list_items` are API-manageable, so rule authoring is either browser-driven or "AI drafts the rule, human pastes it." Live-mode limits are ~100 read + 100 write per second (25/sec in test); Connect writes need platform keys; some Tax registration operations require account enablement first.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 7. Adobe InDesign (Data Merge, GREP styles, preflight, PDF/X) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** InDesign is the only mainstream tool that does variable-data print at scale: paragraph/character/object styles, parent pages, Data Merge, GREP find-change and GREP styles inside paragraph styles, tables, preflight profiles, book files, IDML/XML round-trip and print-grade PDF/X export.

**Why people bounce off it:** The **Data Merge panel and the "Create Merged Document" dialog** — field mapping, multiple-records-per-page, image-path fields — is where the person making 240 product flyers gives up and starts copy-pasting pages by hand. Right behind it is the GREP tab in Find/Change and GREP styles inside a paragraph style (regex, in a design tool, with no preview of what it will match), parent-page overrides, and the style "+" override that silently detaches a paragraph from its style and breaks the document three weeks later. Overset text — the little red plus — is the daily tax.

**AI operator:** A layout-ops agent preloaded with skills for style architecture, data merge, GREP recipes, text-fit and overset repair, preflight, and batch export/package. The high-value framing is not "design for me" — it's "run the 240-variant production job that a designer should never have been doing manually", with a preflight report as the deliverable.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Make 240 listing flyers from this spreadsheet, one per row, keep our template, shrink titles that overset, and give me print-ready PDF/X-4 with bleed."* → The AI maps CSV columns to Data Merge fields including image paths, runs the merged document, applies a GREP style plus auto-size text frames to eliminate overset, runs a preflight profile, exports PDF/X-4 with bleed and packages fonts and links.

**Integration path:** Three surfaces, pick by product shape. ExtendScript (`.jsx`) DOM is the deepest and best-documented; UXP scripting/plugins (InDesign 18.5+) give modern JS with real UI; InDesign Server does headless batch. The one that changes everything is **Adobe Firefly Services "InDesign APIs"** — Adobe-hosted InDesign Server behind REST with S3/Dropbox/Azure asset in and out, i.e. the only path that does **not** require InDesign running on the user's machine (needs a Firefly Services entitlement). Desktop paths mean shipping a local plugin; the Firefly path means shipping SaaS. That choice determines the entire company.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 8. Canva Pro / Teams (Brand Templates, Bulk Create, brand ops) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** 200M+ monthly users, overwhelmingly non-designers. Brand Kit, locked Brand Templates, Bulk Create from CSV, Magic Studio, multi-format resize, team folders and approvals — it is the design tool for people who were never going to open InDesign.

**Why people bounce off it:** **Bulk Create's per-element field connection.** Users understand the concept instantly, then have to right-click each text and image element on the canvas to bind it to a spreadsheet column, and past roughly ten fields they abandon it and make the variants by hand. Brand-template locking rules are the second wall (what a team member can and can't change, and why their edit reverted), resize-to-every-format multiplies the work, and folder/permission chaos arrives the moment a team passes five people.

**AI operator:** A brand-ops operator that owns the whole data-source → variant-generation → naming → foldering → export loop, so nobody ever opens the Bulk Create panel again. Scheduled runs are the real product: the weekly content batch that currently eats a marketing coordinator's Monday.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Every Monday, take new products from our Sheet and make an IG post, a story, and a 16:9 ad from our brand template — on-brand fonts only — drop them in the October folder and send me the links."* → The AI reads the brand-template dataset, fires an autofill job per row, polls each job, creates export jobs (PNG/PDF), moves the designs into the folder and returns edit and view links.

**Integration path:** **Canva Connect APIs** (OAuth2 REST, no desktop needed): `/v1/autofills` against brand-template datasets, `/v1/designs`, `/v1/exports`, `/v1/assets/uploads`, folders, comments. The hard gate: Brand Template and Autofill endpoints require **both the developer and the acting user to be in a Canva Enterprise org** (dev-use exceptions are granted case-by-case), which sharply narrows the initial market to bigger teams. Element-level editing (read/move/restyle items on a page) is only available via the Canva Apps SDK Design Editing API, which runs *inside* the Canva editor — so a complete operator is Connect API plus a companion Canva app. Competitive note: Canva ships its own AI aggressively, so the defensible wedge is the multi-step scheduled pipeline, not single-design generation.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 9. SharePoint Online (effective permissions, sharing links, retention) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** The document layer for most of the corporate world — libraries with versioning and co-authoring, content types and the managed metadata term store, retention labels, item-level permissions, hub sites, search verticals, and lists that back Power Apps and Power Automate.

**Why people bounce off it:** The permission model, and specifically the **"Advanced permissions settings" page**. Broken inheritance on one folder three years ago, SharePoint groups versus M365 Group membership versus Entra security groups versus "Everyone except external users", and sharing links (Anyone / People in your org / Specific people) that silently mint hidden "Limited Access" grants and `SharingLinks.*` system groups. No admin alive can answer *"who can actually see this folder, and by what path?"* from the UI. The secondary wall is the content type hub and term store, which nearly every tenant abandons half-configured.

**AI operator:** Preloaded skills for permission-graph resolution (expand nested groups and sharing links into a real effective-access list per item), sharing-link governance and expiry, site provisioning from templates, retention and sensitivity labelling, and library/content-type/metadata design. Every action is plan → diff → apply with an undo log of the exact Graph and PnP calls made, because the failure mode here is "you just cut 400 people off from the shared drive."

**Killer example:** User says: *"Who outside Finance can see the FY26 Budget folder, and cut them off without breaking the external auditors."* → The AI walks `/drives/{id}/items/{id}/permissions`, expands four nested Entra groups, finds three live "Anyone with the link" links and two guest accounts, revokes the anonymous links, reissues the auditors a specific-people link with a 30-day expiry, restores inheritance on the nine child folders that never needed unique permissions, and prints a before/after access table.

**Integration path:** Microsoft Graph v1.0 (`/sites`, `/drives`, `/lists`, `/permissions`, `createLink`, `invite`) plus SharePoint REST/CSOM (`_api/web`) for what Graph lacks, PnP PowerShell / PnP.Core (`Set-PnPListItemPermission`, site templates, `Get-PnPSiteCollectionAdmin`), and Security & Compliance PowerShell for retention labels. Gotchas: least-privilege app-only access uses `Sites.Selected` with per-site role grants and **there is no Graph call to enumerate which sites you've been granted** — you must track them yourself; Graph throttles with 429 + Retry-After and SPO enforces separate per-app resource units; unique-permission and term-store writes still require CSOM/PnP, so the agent needs a hosted PowerShell runtime alongside its Graph client.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 10. Google Workspace Admin console (OU inheritance, DLP, offboarding) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Runs identity, devices and data for millions of organisations — an OU tree with per-OU service settings, group-targeted policy overrides, context-aware access, Data Protection (DLP) rules with detectors, Drive external-sharing controls, Chrome and ChromeOS policy, Alert Center, and Data Transfer for offboarding.

**Why people bounce off it:** **Policy resolution across the OU tree plus group overrides.** An admin flips a setting under Apps → Google Workspace → Drive and Docs → Sharing settings, it doesn't take effect because a group override wins, and the console offers no "why is this user's effective setting X" view anywhere. The DLP rule builder (scope → conditions → detectors → actions, per app) is the second wall, and offboarding is the third: suspend, transfer Drive, transfer Calendar, sign out sessions, swap to an Archived User licence — a five-step ritual that everyone does three steps of. The operator here is frequently a school IT coordinator or an office manager wearing the IT hat.

**AI operator:** Chat-driven org admin with skills for user lifecycle as a single transaction, effective-policy resolution and explanation, natural-language DLP rules, external-sharing audits, licence reclamation, and audit-log investigation ("who shared externally last week?").

**Killer example:** User says: *"Offboard Priya today — hand her Drive and Calendar to her manager, kill her sessions, keep the mailbox cheap, and stop any external file shares she left open."* → The AI calls Directory API `users.update` (suspended), Data Transfer API `transfers.insert` for Drive and Calendar to the manager, `users.signOut`, swaps her to the Archived User SKU, then sweeps Drive for her externally-shared files and reports the eleven it revoked.

**Integration path:** Admin SDK Directory API (users, orgUnits, groups, members, roles, devices), Data Transfer API, Reports API for audit activities, Groups Settings API, Cloud Identity Groups API, Chrome Policy API, Alert Center API — and the Workspace Policy API, which as of June 2026 added mutate endpoints (create/update/delete) for DLP rules and detectors on top of the previously read-only surface. Auth is OAuth as a super admin, or a service account with domain-wide delegation — and the DWD setup is itself a UX hurdle you must productise. Gotchas: modest default per-project QPS (403 `userRateLimitExceeded` arrives fast on bulk ops), post-write eventual consistency, and real API gaps — Gmail routing/compliance rules have no public API, so scope honestly to what Directory, Chrome Policy and Groups Settings actually cover.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 11. Google Sheets — pivot tables & QUERY() (not Power Query) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Billions of users, and `QUERY()` is a genuine SQL-ish engine running over ranges — plus ARRAYFORMULA, pivot tables with calculated fields, Connected Sheets over BigQuery, filter views and data validation. It is a real analytics tool hiding inside a spreadsheet. (Distinct from the previously-covered Power Query, which is an ETL/M-language surface; this is the in-sheet reporting wall.)

**Why people bounce off it:** `QUERY()` uses the Google Visualization Query Language — `select Col2, sum(Col4) group by Col2 pivot Col3` — with **1-indexed `Col` references and no column names**, so every edit to the source sheet silently breaks it. The pivot-table sidebar is the second wall: Rows / Columns / Values / "Summarize by" / "Show as", plus calculated-field syntax that differs from ordinary cell formulas. Then the ARRAYFORMULA + INDEX/MATCH + IFERROR nesting wall for anything resembling a join across tabs. The person stuck here is in finance or ops, produces this report monthly, and has been doing it by hand for years.

**AI operator:** An operator that talks in business terms, writes the pivot spec and the formula wall, and — the part that matters — **validates by reading the resulting cells back**, self-correcting `#REF!`, `#VALUE!` and wrong-grain errors before handing over. It competes with Gemini-in-Sheets, so the wedge is deliberately not single-formula generation: it's multi-step, multi-tab reconciliation work that spans a whole workbook.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Build a monthly P&L pivot from Transactions, split actual vs budget by account, and flag anything over 10% variance."* → The AI `batchUpdate`s a pivot table (rows = Account, columns = Month, values = SUM(Amount) plus a calculated Variance% value), writes the ARRAYFORMULA lookup into the Budget tab, adds a conditional-format rule for >10%, creates a filter view and protects the formula range — then reads the output back and fixes the two accounts that fell out of the join.

**Integration path:** Sheets API v4 `spreadsheets.batchUpdate` gives full content authoring: `updateCells` carrying a `pivotTable` (including `valueMetadata`, `filterSpecs`, `dataSourcePivotTable`), `addChart`, `addConditionalFormatRule`, `setDataValidation`, `addFilterView`, `addNamedRange`, `addProtectedRange`. Formulas are written as `userEnteredValue.formulaValue`, so authoring QUERY/ARRAYFORMULA is native — no Apps Script required. `addDataSource`/`refreshDataSource` covers Connected Sheets over BigQuery; Drive API handles sharing.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 12. Cisco Meraki Dashboard (group policies, L7 firewall, multi-site) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Cloud-managed switching, wireless, SD-WAN, cameras and MDM behind one dashboard — group policies, per-SSID auth/splash/RADIUS, L3 and L7 firewall plus content filtering, traffic shaping, VLANs, client isolation and network templates. Enormous install base across SMB, retail chains, education and healthcare.

**Why people bounce off it:** Firewall and **group policy semantics**: rule order, Layer 3 versus Layer 7 rules, and the precedence between per-client, per-SSID and per-network policy — plus templates that silently override child networks. The person holding this is a retail chain's regional ops manager or a school's one network guy, and a bad rule takes sixty stores offline at once. The multi-site case is worse: the change is trivial, but doing it 62 times through a web UI is a day of work and one mistake.

**AI operator:** Talk-to-your-network. Preloaded skills to apply and undo policies across many networks atomically, diagnose "wifi is slow at store 14" from real telemetry rather than guesswork, and roll changes site-by-site with an all-or-nothing batch plus automatic revert. The rollback story is the product: what makes a non-specialist willing to type the request at all.

**Killer example:** User says: *"Block TikTok and Netflix on the guest wifi at all 62 stores, but not at HQ, and cap guests to 5 Mbps."* → The AI enumerates the org's networks, builds an action batch of L7 firewall rules plus traffic-shaping rules against each guest SSID (excluding HQ by tag), submits it as one atomic batch, reports the 62 successes/failures, and retains the prior rule sets for a one-command rollback.

**Integration path:** Meraki Dashboard API v1 (`https://api.meraki.com/api/v1`, ~600 endpoints, published OpenAPI spec, official Python/Node SDKs, plus Terraform and Ansible collections). Auth is a bearer key scoped to the admin's own dashboard privileges. Gotchas: **10 requests/sec per organisation** (bursting to ~30 in 2s) with 429 + Retry-After, so bulk work must go through **Action Batches** — atomic, but limited to 5 concurrent batches with a per-batch action cap; and config templates make some per-network endpoints read-only, which the agent must detect rather than fail on.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**
