### 1. Microsoft Intune app protection policies (MAM for personal phones, no enrollment) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Staff can use Outlook, Teams and OneDrive on their own phones while company data stays in a protected container. It blocks copy-paste into WhatsApp, requires an app PIN and allows a selective wipe when someone leaves. None of this needs the device enrolled, which is the realistic BYOD setup for small businesses.

**Why people bounce off it:** Each policy has about 40 settings across data protection, access and conditional launch. You need separate iOS and Android policies, app targeting, group assignment, and a matching Conditional Access grant ("require app protection policy"). Admins routinely ship policies that target no apps, skip Android, or never get enforced because the Conditional Access half is missing. The 2026-08-11 Intune card covered compliance, config profiles, Win32 apps, Autopilot and update rings, not app protection.

**AI operator:** You describe the BYOD rules in plain English. The AI writes paired iOS and Android policies, binds them to the right Microsoft apps, assigns them to groups, and checks for the Conditional Access grant that makes them stick.

**Killer example:** 'Sales can read email and Teams on personal phones, but no copying into WhatsApp, no saving to their own Dropbox, a 6-digit PIN, and wipe company data if they've been offline 30 days.' The AI creates two policies with clipboard limited to managed apps, saving limited to OneDrive for Business and SharePoint, `minimumPinLength` 6 and `periodOfflineBeforeWipeIsEnforced` P30D. It targets Outlook, Teams, OneDrive and Edge, assigns the Sales group, and warns that no Conditional Access policy requires app protection, so Android users can still sign in through the native Gmail app.

**Integration path:** Microsoft Graph v1.0 `POST /deviceAppManagement/iosManagedAppProtections` and `/androidManagedAppProtections`, then the `targetApps` and `assign` actions on `targetedManagedAppProtection` (verified on Learn). Permission `DeviceManagementApps.ReadWrite.All`. The Conditional Access grant goes through `/identity/conditionalAccess/policies`. Overlap caveat: Copilot in Intune lists general "policy and setting management", and Microsoft's Policy Configuration Agent targets the settings catalog, which does not include app protection. Re-check before building.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 2. Exchange Online mailbox archiving (online archive, MRM retention tags, auto-expanding archive, Recoverable Items) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Every Microsoft 365 tenant has policy-driven archiving built in. Mail can move to an archive by age and folder, the archive can grow on its own, and Deleted Items can be emptied on a schedule. It is the standard fix for "mailbox full".

**Why people bounce off it:** Admins confuse three things: Purview retention policies (which keep or delete mail), MRM retention tags (the only way to move mail to the archive), and archive licensing. Purview retention does not even support archive mailboxes, so the legacy MRM path is the one that works. When a mailbox hits its quota and stops receiving mail, admins buy storage instead. Recoverable Items filling up under a hold is a separate, hidden trap. The Exchange cards so far covered mail flow (2026-08-11) and delegation and resource booking (2026-10-21), not archiving. The 2026-10-23 SharePoint/OneDrive card reclaimed file version history; this one moves mailbox content to an archive tier.

**AI operator:** It reports mailbox, archive and Recoverable Items sizes per user. Then it turns a plain-English storage policy into tags and policies, shows projected sizes, applies the change and starts processing.

**Killer example:** 'Sales mailboxes are at 95% of 50 GB. Archive anything older than 2 years for Sales, empty Deleted Items after 30 days for everyone, and leave the CEO alone.' The AI turns on the archive for 23 Sales users and creates a 730-day move-to-archive tag and a 30-day Deleted Items tag. It links both into a "Sales-Archive" policy, assigns it to everyone in Sales except the CEO, and starts the Managed Folder Assistant so mail moves now. It flags 2 mailboxes whose Recoverable Items are full because of a litigation hold that archiving cannot fix.

**Integration path:** Exchange Online PowerShell v3 with app-only certificate auth. Writes: `Enable-Mailbox -Archive`, `Enable-Mailbox -AutoExpandingArchive`, `New-RetentionPolicyTag -Type All|DeletedItems -RetentionAction MoveToArchive|DeleteAndAllowRecovery -AgeLimitForRetention`, `New-RetentionPolicy -RetentionPolicyTagLinks`, `Set-Mailbox -RetentionPolicy`, `Start-ManagedFolderAssistant`. Reads: `Get-MailboxStatistics`, `Get-MailboxFolderStatistics -FolderScope RecoverableItems`. Auto-expanding archive needs Exchange Online Plan 2 or E3 and above. The Microsoft 365 Admin agent covers users, licenses, groups and Teams, not Exchange archiving.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 3. Office Link Fixer: broken Excel/PowerPoint/Word external links after a file-share to SharePoint move `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Finance and operations run on chains of linked workbooks, and on PowerPoint and Word reports that paste live Excel ranges and charts from `\\fileserver\...` paths.

**Why people bounce off it:** After a migration, hundreds of files show "We can't update some of the links" or `#REF!`. Edit Links > Change Source fixes one file at a time, can't see links inside PowerPoint and Word objects, and nobody knows which file feeds which. The 2026-10-04 model-auditing card only flags external links, and the file-share migration planner cut on 2026-10-23 plans the move but does not repair links.

**AI operator:** It crawls a library, builds a map of which file links to which, matches old UNC paths to their new SharePoint URLs, and rewrites the link targets in bulk. It then reports which links resolve and which are still broken.

**Killer example:** 'We moved the S: drive to the Finance SharePoint site last weekend and the month-end pack is broken. Fix every link.' The AI scans 640 files and finds 1,214 links pointing at 38 old paths. It re-points 1,170 of them to the new URLs, including 22 Excel charts linked into the board deck, and lists 44 links whose source workbook was never migrated, grouped by owner.

**Integration path:** Microsoft Graph `GET`/`PUT /drives/{drive-id}/items/{item-id}/content` to round-trip files, with direct edits to the link targets inside the files: Excel external-link and connection parts, PowerPoint OLE links, and Word LINK fields. COM `Workbook.ChangeLink` handles files still on local shares. Office.js `Excel.LinkedWorkbook` only offers `breakLinks()` and `refresh()` (verified), so editing the file itself is the only write path. Competition: desktop link-repair utilities exist, but none map dependencies across a library and repair in bulk.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 4. Salesforce Products, Price Books and revenue/quantity schedules (Pipedrive and Zoho CRM as second connectors) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Products, price books and schedules turn a single deal Amount into product-level, multi-currency, time-phased revenue. Forecasts, ARR reports and the finance handoff all depend on them, and most Sales Cloud orgs use this layer without CPQ.

**Why people bounce off it:** Each currency needs a standard price before any custom price book entry can exist. Multi-year or ramped deals need line-item schedules, and the UI's "Establish Schedule" only splits evenly. Every ramp gets re-split by hand and breaks on the next amount change, so reps skip schedules and the revenue forecast becomes a lump in the close month. The 2026-07-31 Sales Cloud card generated Flow and validation-rule metadata, the 2026-08-23 CPQ card covered CPQ price rules and bundles, and the 2026-10-21 card covered forecasts and quotas. None touched standard price books or schedules.

**AI operator:** It takes deal terms or pricing policy in plain English and writes the records. It computes uneven schedule rows, keeps price book entries consistent across currencies, and checks that schedule totals tie back to the line total.

**Killer example:** 'Acme is $360k over 3 years: 40% due at signing, the rest quarterly, and Year 2 has a 10% uplift.' The AI deletes the even split and writes 1 upfront row plus 11 quarterly `OpportunityLineItemSchedule` rows with the uplift applied. It checks that they sum to the line `TotalPrice` and shows the quarterly revenue table before saving.

**Integration path:** Salesforce REST sObject create/update on `Pricebook2`, `PricebookEntry` and `OpportunityLineItemSchedule` (create/update/upsert/delete confirmed in the object reference). The org needs Products & Schedules enabled and `Product2.CanUseRevenueSchedule` set. "Establish Schedule" has no API, so the operator computes every row itself. Pipedrive: `POST`/`PATCH /v2/deals/{id}/products` with `billing_frequency`, `billing_frequency_cycles` and `billing_start_date` (Growth plan and above). Zoho: `POST /crm/v8/Price_Books`; the payload source is third-party only, so the Zoho connector is caveated.

### 5. Dynamics 365 Business Central RapidStart configuration-package migration (off QuickBooks/Sage/Excel) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Every new Business Central customer migrates from QuickBooks, Sage or Excel. RapidStart configuration packages are the native mechanism for bulk loads and setup: table and field mapping, validation, then apply.

**Why people bounce off it:** Building packages means choosing tables in dependency order, mapping legacy columns, fixing validation errors one by one, and applying them. Go-lives slip on this, and SMBs pay partners to do it. The 2026-08-06 Business Central card was a posting-setup operator and never mentioned migration.

**AI operator:** It reads the legacy exports, builds the packages, imports them into a sandbox company, reads the package errors, fixes them and applies in order. It then reconciles Business Central's trial balance against the source system.

**Killer example:** 'Move us off QuickBooks Desktop: chart of accounts, 1,200 customers, 300 vendors, open AR as of June 30, and the opening trial balance.' The AI generates the packages and validates them against the target company's setup before upload. It catches 37 missing posting groups and payment terms, creates them, then imports and applies. Before anything touches production, it shows the trial balance tying out to the QuickBooks balance sheet to the cent.

**Integration path:** BC automation API with OAuth2 through Entra ID (verified on Learn). `POST /api/microsoft/automation/v2.0/companies({id})/configurationPackages` creates the package; `PATCH .../configurationPackages({id})/file('{code}')/content` uploads it; `POST .../Microsoft.NAV.import` and then `.../Microsoft.NAV.apply` run it, and a GET shows status. No API defines package tables, so the AI generates the .rapidstart file itself. The docs show import and apply returning 204 with no per-record error payload, so validation happens before upload and the trial-balance reconciliation is the final check. Business Central's official MCP server exposes the API pages an admin configures; there is no sign it does package import or apply.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 6. Microsoft Entra cross-tenant access settings (B2B partners, B2B direct connect, M&A) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** For each partner organisation you can set inbound and outbound B2B collaboration, B2B direct connect (Teams shared channels), trust of the partner's MFA and compliant-device claims, and automatic invite redemption.

**Why people bounce off it:** Each organisation has four inbound and outbound tabs, each with separate user, group and application scoping, plus tenant-wide defaults that apply silently. Admins either block everything, which breaks Teams shared channels, or allow everything. Guests then get prompted for MFA twice because inbound trust was never set. The 2026-08-11 Entra card covered Conditional Access, PIM, consent and Access Reviews; the 2026-08-14 Teams admin card covered federation policy, not Entra partner configuration.

**AI operator:** It turns the business relationship into a partner configuration and reports what each side can actually do.

**Killer example:** 'We just acquired Contoso. Their people need our Teams and SharePoint but never Finance. Trust their MFA so they're not prompted twice, and our people shouldn't go into their tenant yet.' The AI creates the Contoso partner entry with inbound B2B collaboration for Office 365 only, `inboundTrust.isMfaAccepted=true`, outbound blocked and automatic redemption on. It excludes Finance through a Conditional Access policy and points out that the tenant default still lets any guest invite anyone.

**Integration path:** Graph v1.0 `POST /policies/crossTenantAccessPolicy/partners` with `Policy.ReadWrite.CrossTenantAccess` and the Security Administrator role (verified on Learn). `PATCH /policies/crossTenantAccessPolicy/default`, `PATCH /policies/authorizationPolicy` (for `allowInvitesFrom`) and `POST /invitations` were not re-checked this run. No Security Copilot agent for cross-tenant settings was found.

### 7. Qlik Sense Enterprise on Windows QMC security rules `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** One attribute-based rule engine controls every stream, app, sheet, data connection, QMC section and license allocation across a multi-node site. It is the governance backbone of a very large on-prem enterprise Qlik base.

**Why people bounce off it:** Rules are raw expressions such as `resource.resourcetype="App" and resource.stream.name="Finance" and user.@department="FP&A"`, with bitmask action values and context flags for Hub, QMC or both. Admins can't predict how overlapping default and custom rules combine, so "why can't Priya see the Finance stream?" becomes a consultant ticket. The 2026-07-31 Qlik card pitched set analysis and load scripts, not security.

**AI operator:** It turns a plain-language access policy into security rules, runs the audit preview against named users and resources before saving, and explains anyone's effective access to anything.

**Killer example:** 'Finance stream: FP&A can publish, the regional managers group can only read apps tagged with their own region, nobody else sees it.' The AI drafts two custom rules with the right resource filter, conditions and action bitmasks, then runs the audit preview for Priya, a regional manager and a random sales user. It shows a who-sees-what matrix, flags a default rule that would still leak read access, and saves only after approval.

**Integration path:** Qlik Repository Service API: `POST /qrs/systemrule` to create, `GET /qrs/systemrule` to read existing rules, and `POST /qrs/systemrule/security/audit/preview` to preview a rule's effect (verified on help.qlik.com, May 2026 docs). Auth is a certificate, or a virtual proxy with JWT or header auth. Qlik's own MCP targets Qlik Cloud analytics; a community `qseow-mcp` server exists but is a raw tool surface, not a policy operator.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 8. Brandfolder (Smartsheet) DAM schema and asset-lifecycle governance `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** An enterprise brand DAM with custom metadata fields, a nested label tree, sections, collections and portals. Each asset has publish and expiry dates plus an approval flag.

**Why people bounce off it:** Setting up custom fields, restructuring labels, and rolling licence-expiry dates out to thousands of assets from a rights spreadsheet all happen one asset or one dialog at a time. Expired stock photos stay live, and the taxonomy drifts as each team adds its own fields.

**AI operator:** A brand-ops admin. It reads the current structure, plans field-schema and lifecycle changes as a dry-run diff, applies them in batches, and watches expiries through webhooks. This is schema and lifecycle work, not AI tagging.

**Killer example:** 'Usage rights for the Q3 shoot are in this sheet. Set each photo's expiry from it, add a required "Licence type" field with Royalty-free / Rights-managed / Owned and fill it, tidy our labels so Social sits under Channels, give the Acme agency access to the Q3 collection, and tell me every Friday what expires next week.' The AI matches sheet rows to assets, sets `availability_end` on 1,180 of them, creates the field and its values, moves the Social label under Channels, and sends the agency a collection invitation. It lists the duplicate "social-media" and "SM" labels with their asset counts as a manual merge, because the API can't re-attach assets to labels. A scheduled job on the operator's side queries upcoming `availability_end` dates every Friday, and webhooks keep its cache current. It flags 14 photos with no matching rights row instead of guessing.

**Integration path:** Brandfolder v4 API (verified in the OpenAPI spec at developers.smartsheet.com), API-token auth. `POST /brandfolders/{id}/custom_field_keys`, `POST /custom_field_keys/{id}/custom_field_values`, `PUT /assets/{id}` (`availability_start`, `availability_end`, `approved`), `POST /brandfolders/{id}/labels`, `PUT /labels/{id}/move`, `POST /collections/{id}/invitations`, `POST /webhooks`. Caveats: no endpoint adds existing assets to a label or collection, and permissions can only be granted through invitations. Smartsheet's official MCP has no Brandfolder tools, and Brandfolder's own AI only auto-tags.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 9. Windows Server Active Directory account hygiene and privileged-group cleanup (on-prem AD DS) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** On-prem AD still holds identity for most SMBs. Users, computers, privileged groups, fine-grained password policies, OU delegation and Windows LAPS decide who can do what on every PC.

**Why people bounce off it:** Active Directory Users and Computers hides what matters: nested membership in Domain Admins, stale accounts, service accounts whose passwords never expire, and computers with no LAPS password. PingCastle and Purple Knight find these problems but fix nothing, so the IT generalist is left with a 60-page PDF. The 2026-08-15 Group Policy and 2026-09-24 AD Certificate Services cards don't touch accounts, groups or delegation.

**AI operator:** A PowerShell-remoting agent on an admin host. It turns "clean up AD" into a staged plan (disable, move to a quarantine OU, delete after N days) with a backup and a dry-run for each step.

**Killer example:** 'Lock down admin rights and clear out the junk before the cyber-insurance audit.' The AI finds 14 inactive users, 52 stale computers, 3 departed staff still nested in Domain Admins through "IT-Old", and a `svc-backup` account with `PasswordNeverExpires` that sits in Enterprise Admins. It disables and quarantines the stale accounts, removes the nested group, adds the real admins to Protected Users, and creates a fine-grained password policy for service accounts. It finishes with the 31 PCs that have no LAPS password.

**Integration path:** ActiveDirectory PowerShell module: `Search-ADAccount -AccountInactive`, `Disable-ADAccount`, `Move-ADObject`, `Get-ADGroupMember -Recursive`, `Remove-ADGroupMember`, `New-ADFineGrainedPasswordPolicy` and `Add-ADFineGrainedPasswordPolicySubject`, plus the LAPS module's `Get-LapsADPassword`. Needs a domain-joined host and delegated rights. These are long-standing cmdlets, not re-checked this run. No Microsoft agent covers on-prem AD.

### 10. Cross-platform UTM, tracking-template and campaign-naming governance (fixes GA4 "Unassigned") `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** GA4 channel reports and every ROAS decision depend on consistent UTMs and campaign names across Google, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok and LinkedIn. One account-level suffix can fix thousands of ads.

**Why people bounce off it:** URL suffixes and tracking templates live at 5 or 6 levels, and lower levels silently override higher ones. Meta `url_tags` sit on creatives that can't be edited. Nobody can trace a GA4 "Unassigned" session back to the ad that caused it. Google's Ask Advisor and Meta's Ads AI Connectors each work inside one platform only.

**AI operator:** It reads GA4 source/medium/campaign breakdowns, matches the bad rows to the live ads that produced them, and writes one taxonomy everywhere: account-level suffixes where supported, campaign renames, and rebuilt Meta creatives where needed.

**Killer example:** '22% of last month's GA4 revenue is Unassigned, fix it.' The AI traces it to Meta ads tagged `utm_medium=social-paid` and Microsoft campaigns with no suffix. It sets a Microsoft account-level suffix and a Google `customer.final_url_suffix`, and renames 41 campaigns to `brand_geo_objective`. After showing a diff, it rebuilds 63 Meta creatives with corrected `url_tags` and repoints each ad.

**Integration path:** Google Ads `CustomerService.MutateCustomer` on `final_url_suffix`/`tracking_url_template` (fields confirmed in the v26 proto) and `CampaignService.MutateCampaigns`. GA4 Data API `runReport` for reads. Meta: `POST /act_{id}/adcreatives`, then `POST /{ad_id}` with the new `creative`; asset-feed and placement-customized creatives may not rebuild cleanly. Microsoft Ads account-level `FinalUrlSuffix` was not re-checked this run. Claravine and UTM.io manage the taxonomy but don't trace and repair live ads.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 11. Jira Cloud structural consolidation (cross-project bulk move, team-managed to company-managed, archiving) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** It is how an org with 200 drifting projects gets back to a manageable set: team projects merge, issue types get re-typed, epics move with their children, and dead history leaves the index.

**Why people bounce off it:** The Bulk Move wizard handles one source issue type at a time, each with its own status-mapping and required-field screens. Team-managed custom fields are scoped to their project, so moves fail until fields are made global. Epics and children move in separate passes and have to be re-linked. Most admins give up and keep the sprawl. Earlier Jira cards covered JQL and bulk edits (2026-07-31), schemes (2026-10-21) and Plans (2026-10-21), not moving work between projects.

**AI operator:** A consolidation planner. It reads the source and target projects and produces a migration plan: issue-type and status mappings, required-field defaults, epic-then-children ordering, and fields to promote first. It runs the plan in batches and finishes by archiving the emptied projects and stale issues.

**Killer example:** 'Fold Mobile-iOS and Mobile-Android (both team-managed) into the company-managed MOBILE project. Keep the epics with their children, and archive anything closed before 2024.' The AI maps Story/Task/Bug and the 7 team-managed statuses onto MOBILE's workflow and flags 3 fields that would be dropped. It moves 41 epics, then their 2,300 children with `parent` set to the new epic IDs. It archives 9,800 old closed issues by JQL, archives both empty projects, and lists any issue that failed to move.

**Integration path:** Verified in Atlassian's official OpenAPI spec: `POST /rest/api/3/bulk/issues/move` with `targetToSourcesMapping` (`targetStatus`, `targetMandatoryFields`, `infer*Defaults`), `POST /rest/api/3/issue/archive` (by JQL, async), `POST /rest/api/3/project/{projectIdOrKey}/archive`, and `POST /rest/api/3/bulk/issues/fields` for clean-up. Caveats: the spec says the move endpoint doesn't yet match the UI's features; whether REST hits the same field-scope failure as the UI is unconfirmed; archiving needs Premium or Enterprise. No sign that Rovo does cross-project moves.

### 12. Adobe InDesign accessible EPUB 3 export for the European Accessibility Act `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** InDesign lays out most trade, education and corporate e-books and can export EPUB 3 that meets the accessibility spec. The EAA has been enforceable since June 2025 and explicitly covers e-books.

**Why people bounce off it:** Adobe has handled the easy part: InDesign 21.2 to 21.5 added AI alt text, ARIA roles and table headers. The rest is still manual: mapping every paragraph and character style to semantic tags, building reading order in the Articles panel, marking running heads and decorations as artifacts, and filling in the accessibility metadata. Then Ace by DAISY fails and nobody knows which InDesign setting caused it. The 2026-08-13 InDesign card covered data merge, GREP and preflight; the Acrobat cards cover PDF/UA.

**AI operator:** A backlist accessibility operator that loops export, Ace check, fix, re-export, using Ace by DAISY and EPUBCheck as the pass/fail judge.

**Killer example:** 'Get our 40-title backlist ready for the EAA. Every EPUB must pass Ace.' For each title the AI maps 60 styles to semantic tags with `epubType` roles, builds the Articles in reading order, marks folios and decorative rules as artifacts, writes the accessibility metadata, then exports and runs Ace. It reports 37 passing and queues 3 for a person: two with image-only tables and one fixed-layout spread.

**Integration path:** InDesign scripting DOM (verified at indesignjs.de, "indesign-latest"): `ParagraphStyle.styleExportTagMaps.add(...)`, `Document.articles.add(...)` and `Article.articleMembers.add(pageItem)`, `ObjectExportOption.applyTagType` (including `TAG_ARTIFACT`), `epubType`, `customAltText`, and the `EPubExportPreference` accessibility fields (mode, feature, hazard, summary, conformsTo). UXP scripting also works. The Ace by DAISY CLI and EPUBCheck handle verification. Caveat: InDesign's AI Assistant beta lists copy, styling and print-readiness, not accessibility, but Adobe keeps adding accessibility natively.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**
