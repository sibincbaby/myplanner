### 1. Chargebee (subscription billing, revenue & RevRec) — 'Pricing Ops Operator' `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** A full revenue engine behind thousands of SaaS/subscription finance teams: Product Catalog 2.0 item families/items/item prices with multi-currency price points, proration, ramps, tiered and usage-based pricing, coupon stacking, dunning with smart retries, native + Avalara tax, entitlements, deferred-revenue and RevRec reporting, and GL sync into QuickBooks/NetSuite/Xero. Its REST API writes the entire catalog and subscription surface, and a free test site is a true sandbox — so a solo dev gets a realistic, fully-writable environment on day one with zero gatekeeping.

**Why people bounce off it:** Post-Catalog-2.0 the model is a schema, not a form: item family → item → item price → attached addons/charges, with proration flags, end_of_term vs immediate changes, and changes_scheduled_at semantics that decide whether a customer gets billed today or at renewal. A 'simple' price increase becomes price-point creation + cohort selection + grandfathering + invoice regeneration + notice emails, with no safe bulk path in the UI — so finance can't execute pricing changes alone and every one becomes a multi-week engineering ticket. Mistakes charge real customers real money, which makes people freeze: the observed behaviour is companies leaving pricing unchanged for years because nobody will own the migration.

**AI operator:** A plan→simulate→apply pipeline over the REST API. It compiles a plain-language pricing instruction into a migration plan (cohort query over subscription filters, per-subscription update payloads, effective dates, proration flags, exclusions), estimates ARR/MRR impact from current state, executes first against the free test site, then applies in idempotent batches with a rollback ledger and live progress diff. The differentiator over the UI is that the risky parts — who is in the cohort, when each customer actually gets charged, who is mid-ramp or in dunning — are surfaced as counted, reviewable facts before a single write. Skills also cover dunning policy, coupon/entitlement changes, and reconciling the deferred-revenue report against the GL sync.

**Killer example:** CFO to the ops lead on a Tuesday: 'Raise Growth-annual from $1,000 to $1,200 for everyone who signed up after Jan 1, effective at their next renewal, no proration — but keep LEGACY20 coupon holders at their current rate, and send the 30-day notice.' The operator creates the new item_price, segments the subscription list, writes per-subscription renewal-effective changes with prorate=false, excludes coupon holders, and dry-runs the whole batch on the free test site. It comes back in four minutes: '412 subscriptions, +$82.4K ARR. 7 conflicts I will not touch without you: 3 are mid-ramp and this would overwrite month 2 of their ramp schedule, 4 are in active dunning and repricing them now restarts the retry cycle. Also 11 customers signed up Jan 1 in their local timezone but Dec 31 UTC — include or exclude?' On approval it executes in idempotent batches of 50 with a rollback ledger, queues the notice emails, and hands back a per-customer CSV finance can hand to support. The alternative was a six-week engineering ticket nobody wanted to file.

**Integration path:** Chargebee REST API v2 with full CRUD on items, item_prices, subscriptions, customers, coupons, invoices and credit notes; idempotency keys for safe batch writes; webhooks/events for post-apply verification; official SDKs (Node/Python/Java/Ruby); a free test site gives a solo dev a complete, realistic environment to build and demo against without a single customer. No approval queue, no partner program, no sales call — rare on this list.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 2. Microsoft Excel Solver + Analysis ToolPak + What-If Analysis — 'Optimizer Copilot' `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Every Excel install on earth ships a genuine constrained-optimization engine (Frontline's Simplex LP, GRG Nonlinear, Evolutionary GA) plus a real statistics package (regression, ANOVA, t-tests, moving average, exponential smoothing, correlation matrices, histograms) and a scenario/sensitivity engine. This is operations-research and econometrics tooling — production scheduling, blending, crew rostering, portfolio allocation, shipping-route cost minimization, price elasticity regression — sitting inside a tool that 800M+ people already have and already hold their data in. No procurement, no Python, no new vendor.

**Why people bounce off it:** Both add-ins are OFF by default, so most users never learn they exist. For those who find Solver, the wall is model formulation, not clicking: you must physically lay out the spreadsheet as decision-variable cells, an objective formula cell, and constraint formula cells BEFORE the Solver dialog is useful — the dialog cannot express 'each machine runs at most 16 hours' unless you already built the SUMPRODUCT row for it. Then comes solving-method choice (pick GRG on a model with IF/MIN/ROUND and you get a wrong local optimum silently), opaque failures ('Solver could not find a feasible solution' with no pointer to which constraint), integer/binary constraints via a cryptic relation dropdown, the hidden 'Make Unconstrained Variables Non-Negative' checkbox that changes answers, and Options tabs nobody can reason about. Analysis ToolPak is equally brutal: modal dialogs demanding contiguous Input Y / Input X ranges (X columns must be adjacent — a killer), and output that is a static unlabeled dump with no diagnostics. Result: people rebuild optimization as guess-and-check manual tinkering, or hire a consultant.

**AI operator:** An Excel task-pane add-in that turns a plain-English business problem into a fully built, auditable Solver model on the user's own sheet. Pipeline: read the workbook's used ranges, tables and headers to infer what the data means; the LLM produces a structured model spec (decision variables + cell block, objective sense/cell, typed constraint expressions, variable types, solver method) — never raw prose; a deterministic builder materializes that spec into real labeled cells with named ranges and SUMPRODUCT formulas so every step is visible and auditable; it drives the solve; then it explains the result and runs sensitivity automatically (Answer/Sensitivity reports, Data Tables, Scenario Manager). It owns the failure modes: on infeasibility it runs an IIS-style relaxation search and names the conflicting constraints in business terms; on nonlinearity it detects IF/MAX/ABS/ROUND in the objective chain and either rewrites to linear big-M form or explains the engine switch. Ships a validation harness — re-solve with HiGHS/CBC in a sidecar and flag disagreement — so users are protected from silent GRG local optima.

**Killer example:** A bakery owner with one messy sheet types: 'I have 40 oven hours, 500 kg of flour and 2 decorators for 30 hours next week, I can't sell more than 200 of anything, and I've already promised 150 wedding cupcakes — what should I bake to make the most money?' The add-in writes a visible Production Plan block on her sheet (B12:G12 named Qty, objective =SUMPRODUCT(Qty,Margin), four labeled constraint rows), sets integer constraints, picks Simplex LP, solves, and answers in her language: 'Bake 200 croissants, 150 wedding cupcakes, 88 sourdough → ₹2.14L margin, ₹31k more than last week's mix. Oven time is what's holding you back: one extra oven hour is worth ₹1,850, so renting the neighbour's oven at ₹900/hr pays until you hit 7 extra hours, then it stops helping. Flour isn't the problem — you'll have 61 kg spare, stop over-ordering it.' It leaves the Sensitivity Report and a margin-vs-oven-hours Data Table on the sheet, and cross-checks the answer against HiGHS before showing it. Every formula is on the sheet, so her accountant can audit it — which is exactly why she'll trust it over a black-box web app.

**Integration path:** Two real surfaces. (a) Desktop Excel: Solver exposes a documented VBA function library — SolverReset, SolverOk(SetCell, MaxMinVal, ValueOf, ByChange, Engine), SolverAdd(CellRef, Relation, FormulaText) with Relation 5/4/6 = bin/int/alldifferent, SolverOptions, SolverSolve, SolverFinish, SolverGet, plus Answer/Sensitivity report generation — callable from VBA, a COM add-in, or Python via xlwings/pywin32 (Application.Run 'SOLVER.XLAM!SolverOk'), which is the cleanest solo-dev path. Analysis ToolPak similarly via Application.Run 'ATPVBAEN.XLAM!Regression'. (b) Excel on the web: Office.js can't call Solver, so build the model as cells and solve in a sidecar (HiGHS-WASM or FastAPI + PuLP/OR-Tools) — which gives the cross-checking solver for free and a Google Sheets fallback. MVP: xlwings add-in on Windows desktop, structured model spec as the LLM contract, HiGHS as verifier.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 3. SQL Server Analysis Services Tabular / Azure Analysis Services semantic models (XMLA + TMSL, Tabular Editor) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** The tabular model is the shared source of truth an entire company consumes: Excel pivot users connect via Analyze in Excel, paginated reports and dashboards sit on the same measures, and VertiPaq compresses hundreds of millions of rows into sub-second queries. It supports row/object-level security, calculation groups, composite/DirectQuery models, perspectives, translations and incremental-refresh partitions — an enormously capable layer with a fully scriptable metadata object model (TMSL/TOM) and zero UI automation anywhere in the loop.

**Why people bounce off it:** The wall is DAX filter context plus model engineering. CALCULATE semantics, context transition, ALLSELECTED vs ALL, bidirectional relationship ambiguity, calculation-group precedence, and RLS filter expressions defeat competent finance and ops analysts — the classic symptom is 'my YoY% is right at company level and wrong when I slice by two dimensions' with no way to diagnose it. Authoring happens in a Visual Studio project or a field pane, partition/refresh policy management is a separate discipline, and the practical result is that every model change queues behind a two-person BI team for weeks while the business builds shadow spreadsheets that then disagree with the official numbers.

**AI operator:** A headless model-ops agent that connects to the XMLA endpoint, pulls the full TMSL/TOM metadata (tables, relationships, measure DAX, roles, partitions) as context, and turns plain-language requests into measure DAX, calculation groups, RLS roles or partition changes. Critically it self-verifies before promoting: it deploys to a dev model, executes DAX EVALUATE queries over ADOMD, and proves control totals still tie — or explains exactly what changed and why, to the rupee. It also runs Best Practice Analyzer rules, and answers 'why is this number wrong' by decomposing an existing measure's filter context into plain English, which is the single most-asked and least-answerable question in the BI world.

**Killer example:** A finance manager, three days from board pack: 'Net revenue should exclude intercompany, each regional controller should only see their own region, and I need a 3-month rolling average.' The agent reads the live model, writes Net Revenue := CALCULATE([Revenue], KEEPFILTERS(Sales[IsIntercompany]=FALSE)) and a DATESINPERIOD rolling measure, adds an RLS role filtering Region via LOOKUPVALUE over USERPRINCIPALNAME(), and pushes a TMSL createOrReplace to the dev model over XMLA. Then it does the thing the BI team never has time for: it runs EVALUATE proving FY Q3 now equals the old total minus exactly 4.2M of intercompany — matching the GL elimination entry to the rupee — and separately flags that the existing YoY% measure uses ALL(Date) instead of ALLSELECTED, which is why her slicer-by-region-and-product number has been wrong for two quarters and nobody could explain it. It presents the TMDL diff for approval and only then promotes to prod. Two weeks in the BI queue, collapsed to one afternoon, with proof instead of trust.

**Integration path:** XMLA endpoint speaking TMSL JSON commands (createOrReplace / alter / refresh) via AMO/TOM (Microsoft.AnalysisServices.Tabular NuGet, usable from Python through pythonnet); Tabular Editor 2 CLI (free and open source) for scripted deploy, C# model-manipulation scripts and Best Practice Analyzer; model metadata as .bim/TMDL files in git for reviewable diffs; DAX queries through ADOMD or the executeQueries REST call for verification. No UI automation anywhere. Caveat to design around: on Power BI/Fabric capacities XMLA write requires Premium/Fabric SKU, while SSAS on-prem (free developer edition) and Azure AS have no such gate — so build against SSAS developer edition for free.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 4. AWS EventBridge + Step Functions (event patterns, input transformers, Pipes, Amazon States Language) — merged serverless orchestration operator `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Together these are the nervous system and the spine of every serverless AWS estate. EventBridge: ~200 AWS services emit events natively, content-based filtering routes to 20+ target types with no code, Pipes does source→filter→enrich→target, Scheduler runs cron at millions-of-schedules scale, Archive+Replay re-drives yesterday's traffic through a fixed consumer. Step Functions: durable orchestration with 10k+ parallel branches via Distributed Map, direct SDK integrations with 220+ services (no glue Lambdas), built-in Retry/Catch with exponential backoff, wait-for-callback task tokens, year-long executions, versions + aliases for traffic-shifted rollout, and full execution history for audit. One team, one buyer, one connector — they are the same product surface in practice.

**Why people bounce off it:** Both hinge on opaque JSON mini-languages whose failure mode is silence. EventBridge patterns are not JSONPath and not regex — shape-matching with $or, anything-but, numeric [">",500], prefix/suffix, exists and wildcard — and a wrong pattern simply never fires, with nothing in the console explaining why. Input transformers are a second language with quoting rules that break on nested JSON. Step Functions' real artifact is Amazon States Language, where InputPath/Parameters/ResultSelector/ResultPath/OutputPath chaining (now doubled by the newer JSONata + Variables mode), $.Payload unwrapping, per-state Retry/Catch matrices of error strings, and Distributed Map's ItemBatcher/ToleratedFailurePercentage are all invisible in Workflow Studio's drag-and-drop view. So the first real workflow dies on 'The JSONPath $.detail.items could not be found' after a 12-minute run, DLQs are off by default, and every team ends up with one person who 'knows Step Functions' while everyone else routes around it with polling and cron.

**AI operator:** An event-and-workflow operator whose defining feature is that AWS already ships free correctness oracles nobody uses, and the agent wires them into a loop. Describe the process in English; the agent emits the pattern or the ASL, then proves it before deploying: TestEventPattern against real events pulled from the bus Archive ('matches 43 of your last 4,102 events — here are 3 that matched and 3 near-misses that didn't and why'), ValidateStateMachineDefinition for static diagnostics, TestState to run a single state with real input and the real IAM role so JSONPath and permission errors surface in seconds instead of after a 12-minute run, Step Functions Local + MockConfigFile for a full mocked dry run, input-transformer output rendered before commit. It also owns the reliability checklist humans skip — DLQ, retry policy, max event age, FailedInvocations alarms — and the forensic half: read GetExecutionHistory on a failure, name in English which state ate the event and why, patch the Retry policy or path expression, publish a new version, shift the alias, and RedriveExecution the failed run.

**Killer example:** 3am page, checkout orders silently not reaching fulfillment. The engineer types: 'Nothing has hit the fulfillment queue since the deploy — what broke, and while you're there I want orders over $500 from prod payments routed there, never from our test tenants, sending just customer id and amount.' The agent replays 500 events from the Archive, diffs each against the live rule field by field, and answers: 'Your rule still requires detail-type "OrderCompleted" but yesterday's deploy renamed it to "checkout.completed" — the rule has matched 0 of 4,102 events since 19:40 and there was no DLQ, so 1,106 orders are only recoverable from the archive.' It writes the corrected pattern (detail-type match, detail.amount numeric >500, detail.tenant anything-but the three test ids), proves it with TestEventPattern over the archive — 43 real orders caught, 118 test orders correctly skipped — renders the exact transformed JSON the queue will receive, attaches an SQS DLQ with 24h max event age and 5 retries plus a FailedInvocations alarm so this can never be silent again, then offers a Step Functions Distributed Map to replay the 1,106 missed orders at 50 concurrency, tested with TestState on row 1 first. Finishes with the CDK diff for the repo. That whole night was, before this, a four-hour console archaeology session.

**Integration path:** Entirely public API, no scraping. EventBridge: PutRule/PutTargets/DescribeRule (EventPattern + InputTransformer + DeadLetterConfig + RetryPolicy), TestEventPattern as a free correctness oracle, CreateArchive/StartReplay for historical validation, ListSchemas/DescribeSchema + Discoverer, CreatePipe, CreateSchedule, CloudWatch metrics (TriggeredRules, FailedInvocations, InvocationsSentToDlq). Step Functions: CreateStateMachine/UpdateStateMachine/PublishStateMachineVersion/UpdateStateMachineAlias, ValidateStateMachineDefinition, TestState, StartExecution/DescribeExecution/GetExecutionHistory/RedriveExecution, plus the stepfunctions-local Docker image for mocked dry runs. Ships as a CLI + MCP server the user's coding agent calls, or a small web app using a customer-provided cross-account IAM role; optional emit-to-git mode writes CDK/SAM/Terraform instead of clicking prod. Read-only scopes for diagnose mode, write scopes behind a diff preview. Solo-dev MVP is a weekend over boto3; the upsell is the hosted archive-backed 'would this rule have fired?' simulator, which nothing in the AWS console does across historical traffic.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 5. Mozilla Thunderbird (message filters, virtual folders, tags, quick filter) — 'Mailwright' `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** Thunderbird is the default mail client for tens of millions — Linux desktops, European public sector, universities, small businesses that refuse cloud mail, anyone running IMAP against their own server — and it now ships on Android. Its filtering engine is genuinely stronger than Gmail's or Outlook's: unlimited rules per account with full condition trees, matching on any header including custom X- headers, body, age-in-days, size, tags, junk score, priority and 'from is/isn't in my address book'; actions include move/copy across accounts, tag, star, set priority, forward, reply-with-template, delete and 'Stop filter execution'; filters can run on manual trigger, after junk classification, on archive, or on send. On top sit cross-account saved-search virtual folders and a huge add-on ecosystem. It is a free local mail-automation engine attached to your own mailbox.

**Why people bounce off it:** The Message Filters UI is a 2004-era modal stack that actively defeats people. Filters are per-account and silently do nothing if built on the wrong one; conditions are added one row at a time in tiny dropdowns and the entire rule is all-AND or all-OR, so any real rule like '(from my bank OR subject has invoice) AND older than 30 days' must be split into several correctly-ordered filters. Order matters enormously and is managed by Move Up/Move Down with no simulation; 'Stop filter execution' is the only control flow and its interaction with later rules is invisible. There is no dry-run, no 'which filter touched this message?', and 'Run Now' irreversibly moves mail. Filters only run automatically on newly arriving inbox mail, so users are baffled when rules ignore existing folders. The universal outcome: people declare bankruptcy on 40k unread messages, or hand-sort forever.

**AI operator:** A Thunderbird add-on that makes the filtering engine conversational and — the real differentiator — safe. Three moves. (1) Mine, don't ask: on install it analyses the mailbox locally (senders, List-Id/List-Unsubscribe headers, thread density, read rates, existing folders) and proposes a concrete rule set with numbers attached, so the user is approving evidence rather than inventing rules. (2) NL → rules with a compiler: the LLM emits a structured rule spec, a deterministic compiler lowers it into Thunderbird's real filter model — splitting mixed AND/OR into correctly ordered multi-filter chains with the right 'Stop execution' placement, exactly the part humans get wrong — then writes the live filter list. (3) Dry-run first, always: before writing anything it evaluates proposed rules against existing mail via messages.query and reports the blast radius with samples and suspected false positives, with one-click undo because every move is logged. Falls out cheaply: 'explain why this message ended up here' replays the rule chain, unsubscribe sweeps via List-Unsubscribe, virtual-folder generation, exportable rule sets for teams.

**Killer example:** A freelancer with 41,000 unread messages types one sentence: 'Stop the bleeding — file anything from my accountant or with an invoice PDF into Finance, tag anything from a @clientco.com address as Client and mark it important, dump every newsletter I haven't opened in 3 months into Archive/Newsletters, and never touch mail from my wife.' Mailwright returns a plan of 7 filters in a specific order, with the wife-exclusion compiled as filter #1 carrying Stop execution — the ordering subtlety that makes the difference between 'works' and 'quietly archived my anniversary plans', and the exact thing a human building this by hand gets wrong. Before writing anything it replays the whole chain against the existing mailbox: '2,847 messages would move. Here are 25 samples. Three look wrong to me: your accountant's domain is also used by a marketing list, and these 14 invoice-PDF matches are actually receipts from a food delivery app — exclude?' On approve it backs up msgFilterRules.dat, writes the filters, runs them over existing mail with a reversible transaction log, and creates a 'Client — needs reply' virtual folder. Inbox goes from 41,000 to 340 in ninety seconds, undoable.

**Integration path:** Solid, with one honest gap. Thunderbird 115+ MailExtensions give first-class APIs for the hard parts: messages.query/list/update/move/copy/archive/tags, folders.create, accounts, messageDisplay/compose, browser.storage — covering mailbox analysis, dry-run simulation, bulk application and undo entirely through supported APIs. Filters have no stable public WebExtension API yet, so rule persistence goes through either the documented plain-text msgFilterRules.dat in the profile (well-understood format, existing parsers and CLI tools) or an Experiment API touching nsIMsgFilterService/nsIMsgFilterList, the approach quickFilters and FiltaQuilla already use. Pragmatic MVP: ship 'analyze + dry-run + bulk apply' purely on supported APIs — fully useful on day one with no filter writing at all — then add persistence. LLM calls go to a user-supplied key or a local model, which matters because this audience self-hosts for privacy. Distribution through addons.thunderbird.net reaches the install base with zero marketing spend.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 6. Microsoft Lists (column & view formatting JSON, calculated columns, validation, conditional show/hide, rules) — 'Lists Designer' `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Microsoft Lists is the 'no-code database' shipped to every M365 tenant and surfaced inside Teams, and it's how tens of millions of non-developers actually run onboarding trackers, issue logs, asset registers, request queues and approval pipelines. Its declarative formatting layer is startlingly capable — column and view formatting JSON can render data bars, colored status pills, conditional icons, KPI roll-ups, images, progress bars, hover cards, custom card layouts in Gallery/Board views, and inline action buttons that launch a Flow, send email, or open a filtered view — all rendered natively in the browser and Teams. Add formula-based conditional show/hide on forms, column validation, calculated columns and per-list rules, and a competent builder produces something that looks like a bespoke internal app in an afternoon.

**Why people bounce off it:** All of that power is gated behind hand-written JSON the UI barely helps with. 'Format this column' opens a pane whose advanced mode is a raw textarea — no schema autocomplete, no element picker, no inspector — where you must know the exact schema ($schema, elmType, txtContent, style, attributes, children, forEach) and an idiosyncratic Excel-ish expression dialect with two incompatible syntaxes (legacy nested operatorName/operands vs the newer '=if(...)' string form), its own function set (getUserImage, @currentField, @now, @me, toLocaleDateString), and a hard requirement to use INTERNAL field names — so a column displayed as 'Due Date' must be referenced as Due_x0020_Date, which nobody discovers unaided. Errors are silent: a typo yields a blank cell or an unlocated 'invalid JSON', and there is no debugger. The entire ecosystem runs on copy-paste from the pnp/List-Formatting samples repo, and the moment a user needs 'like that sample but with my four status values' they stall. Conditional show/hide is a second undocumented expression language, validation formulas a third.

**AI operator:** An operator that turns a spoken description of how a list should look and behave into validated formatting JSON and applies it, with a rendered preview before commit. Three parts: (1) a schema-grounded generator that reads the actual list's columns via Graph/SharePoint REST — types, internal names, real choice values — so it emits correct [$Internal_x0020_Name] references and enumerates the user's actual statuses instead of hallucinating them, which is the single largest source of failure; (2) a hard validator that checks output against the published formatting JSON schema, resolves every field reference against the live list, and lints the classic failure modes (mixed legacy/new syntax, missing $schema, date comparisons as strings, forEach misuse) — the LLM never writes to the tenant, the validator gates it; (3) a preview renderer that replays the JSON against real sample rows in the tool's own UI, with one-click revert to the previously stored CustomFormatter. Beyond formatting it covers the sibling expression languages: validation formulas, conditional show/hide, calculated columns and list rules.

**Killer example:** A facilities manager with no dev access, staring at a grey Equipment Requests list, describes what she wants in one breath: 'Make Status show as colored pills — green Approved, amber Pending, red Rejected; turn the whole row light red if Due Date is in the past and it's not Approved; show the requester's photo next to their name; add a button on each row that emails the requester. And don't show Rejection Reason on the form unless Status is Rejected.' The operator fetches the real column schema first, discovers her Status choices are actually 'Approved', 'Awaiting parts' and 'Declined' — not the words she said — and maps them, then generates a view formatter with rowFormatter, a Status column formatter, a Person formatter using getUserImage(), a button elmType with a mailto customRowAction, and the show/hide formula. It validates all four against the published schema and her list's internal names (Due_x0020_Date, not 'Due Date'), renders a live preview against 5 of her real rows so she sees the pills and the red overdue rows before anything changes, and stores the old CustomFormatter for rollback. Her list goes from a spreadsheet to something that looks like a bought product, in four minutes, with no ticket to IT.

**Integration path:** Clean and multi-surface. Read the list schema and items with Microsoft Graph (/sites/{id}/lists/{id}/columns, /items) using an Entra app registration with delegated Sites.Manage.All — standard OAuth, no admin drama for a single-user install. Write formatting via SharePoint REST/CSOM: column formatting lives in the field's CustomFormatter property (_api/web/lists/getbytitle('X')/fields('Y') via MERGE) and view formatting in the view's CustomFormatter; PnP PowerShell's Set-PnPField -CustomFormatter / Set-PnPView -CustomFormatter document the exact shape, and pnp/script-samples has working export/import scripts. Validation is free — Microsoft publishes the JSON schemas, and pnp/List-Formatting is a ready-made few-shot corpus of hundreds of verified samples. Delivery, easiest first: a standalone web app the user signs into with their M365 account (fastest MVP, no tenant deployment), an SPFx form-customizer for in-page UX, or a Teams app. Preview rendering reuses the declarative semantics in a small React interpreter. Solo-dev MVP is realistically a weekend plus the validator.

### 7. Google Business Profile (multi-location local marketing) — 'local presence operator' `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** For any business with a physical footprint it is the highest-ROI marketing surface there is: hours and special holiday hours, 4,000+ categories and per-category attributes, services and menus, products, photos, Q&A, review replies, local posts (now including recurring posts), UTM-tagged website links, and the Performance API's call/direction/search-term metrics — all feeding the map pack that drives actual foot traffic and phone calls. For a 300-location franchise this surface is worth more than their paid search budget.

**Why people bounce off it:** Everything above is per-location clicking in a UI built for one coffee shop, while real users have 12, 80 or 400 locations: setting Thanksgiving hours means 400 edit flows; a category change that would lift rankings has to be repeated everywhere; reviews arrive constantly and an unanswered 1-star is both a ranking tax and a conversion tax; edits get silently reverted by 'Google updates' or trip a suspension requiring reinstatement evidence; Location Groups help but the bulk spreadsheet upload is its own arcane schema. Franchise marketers, dental/HVAC/restaurant groups and local SEO agencies do this by hand or pay $30-100/location/month to Yext-class tools — which is a very visible, very re-biddable line item.

**AI operator:** One agent with the GBP API skillset plus a brand-voice and review-escalation policy config. It answers plain-language requests across the whole portfolio (bulk hour/attribute/category edits, post campaigns targeted by region or store attribute), drafts and posts review replies with escalation rules (anything mentioning injury, refunds over $X, or staff by name goes to a human), watches for reverted edits and re-applies them, and turns the Performance API into causal English: 'these 9 stores lost direction requests after the category change — here's the fix.' Because bulk edits and reply drafting are precisely the work humans hate and agencies bill for, retention is structurally high and the value is measurable in the client's own dashboard.

**Killer example:** It is the Friday before Thanksgiving week and a franchise marketer with 312 locations types: 'Set Thanksgiving hours — closed everywhere except the 14 airport stores which run 6am-2pm, push the pumpkin-latte promo post to all Northeast stores for the next three Mondays, and clear the review backlog.'
