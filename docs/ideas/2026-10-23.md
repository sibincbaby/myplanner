### 1. Google Ads offline conversion import + enhanced conversions for leads (Data Manager API) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Feeding real CRM outcomes (qualified leads, closed deals, revenue) back into Google Ads is what lets Smart Bidding optimize for profit instead of form fills. For lead-gen advertisers it is the biggest lever after the budget itself.

**Why people bounce off it:** It is pure plumbing. You capture the GCLID on every form, store it in the CRM, create the right conversion actions, hash user data for enhanced conversions, and upload on a schedule inside the conversion window. Most SMBs and agencies never finish it. Since 15 June 2026, Google Ads API offline imports are blocked in favour of the Data Manager API, so every existing homegrown uploader has to be rebuilt. The 2026-08-11 GTM card handled the tag side and the 2026-10-22 card handled conversion value rules; neither moved CRM outcomes back into Ads.

**AI operator:** It connects to Google Ads and the CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive). It creates the conversion actions, checks that auto-tagging and GCLID capture work end to end, maps CRM stages to conversions with values, and runs the recurring upload with match-rate diagnostics.

**Killer example:** 'Count a lead as converted when HubSpot marks it SQL, worth $400, and count Closed-Won at deal value.' The AI creates two offline conversion actions, maps both lifecycle stages, backfills 90 days of GCLID and hashed-email events, and schedules a daily sync. It then reports a 62% match rate with the cause: 'your Typeform embed drops the GCLID.'

**Integration path:** Google Ads API `ConversionActionService.MutateConversionActions` (type `UPLOAD_CLICKS`). Data Manager API `ingestEvents` with `adIdentifiers.gclid`, GA since v1.3, Oct 2025 (developers.google.com/data-manager/api/devguides/events/google-ads/offline/send-events, verified). CRM side uses standard REST and webhooks. Google's in-product AI can't pull data out of a third-party CRM.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 2. Jira Service Management customer portal + Forms (request types, conditional forms, customer organizations) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** JSM Forms adds conditional sections, Jira-field linking and per-request-type intake on top of the help-center portal. Customer organizations add B2B ticket sharing. Together they turn a generic service desk into a structured intake system.

**Why people bounce off it:** Admins have to wire request types, form designs (sections, conditions, field links), portal groups and organization sharing across four separate admin screens. Most portals end up as a single 'General request' with a description box. The 2026-07-31 Jira card covered workflows, JQL and Automation; the 10-15 card covered Assets. Neither covered intake design.

**AI operator:** It turns a plain-English intake spec into request types, form templates with conditional logic and field links, and customer organizations. It shows a preview of what the customer will see before publishing.

**Killer example:** 'Laptop requests: ask new or replacement; if replacement, require the asset tag and a damage photo; each client company sees only its own tickets.' The AI creates the request type and a form with a conditional 'Replacement' section linked to the Asset Tag field, and publishes it to that request type. It then creates one organization per client, attaches the customers and adds the organizations to the service desk.

**Integration path:** Forms REST API `POST /project/{projectIdOrKey}/form` with a `design` object that includes `conditions`; since Feb 2026 `PUT` also saves advanced conditional logic. Forms attach to request types in the same create/save body via `publish.portal.portalRequestTypeIds` and `publish.jira.issueCreateRequestTypeIds` (developer.atlassian.com/cloud/forms/rest/api-group-forms-on-project, verified). Request types, organizations and customers use the JSM REST API (`/rest/servicedeskapi/servicedesk/{id}/requesttype`, `/organization`, `/customer`). Atlassian's 'Suggest Fields' only suggests fields; it doesn't build conditional forms.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 3. Microsoft Entra app provisioning (SCIM attribute mappings, expressions, scoping filters) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** It automates joiner/mover/leaver for every connected SaaS app, and for HR-driven inbound provisioning from Workday or SuccessFactors. It is the identity plumbing behind most Microsoft-shop SaaS estates.

**Why people bounce off it:** Mapping expressions (Switch, IIF, Join, NormalizeDiacritics), scoping-filter clauses, schema editing and matching precedence are all hand-written in a cramped UI. Mistakes silently mis-provision or deprovision people. MemberOf scoping support ends 3 Nov 2026, which forces rework in many tenants. The seen Entra card (2026-08-11) covered Conditional Access, PIM, consent and Access Reviews, not provisioning.

**AI operator:** It writes and debugs mapping expressions and scoping filters from plain English. It dry-runs them on sample users with provision-on-demand and explains provisioning-log failures in plain language.

**Killer example:** 'Only provision Sales and Support users in India to Zendesk, build the username as first.last in lowercase without accents, and stop syncing contractors.' The AI edits the synchronization schema (scoping filter plus an expression mapping) and runs provision-on-demand for 3 test users. It shows the before and after attributes for each, then commits.

**Integration path:** Microsoft Graph `PUT /servicePrincipals/{id}/synchronization/jobs/{jobId}/schema` (full replace), `POST .../provisionOnDemand`, `POST .../restart`, `GET /auditLogs/provisioning`. The Entra Security Copilot agents cover access reviews, app discovery and tenant health, not mappings.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 4. SharePoint Online / OneDrive version-history storage reclaim (version limits, batch trim jobs) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Version history is the main driver of tenant storage overages, and overages are billed per GB. Microsoft now exposes automatic version expiry, count limits and batch trim jobs that can reclaim terabytes.

**Why people bounce off it:** These controls exist only as PowerShell cmdlets, and the deletes can't be undone. Admins don't know which sites are bloated or what a trim would remove, so they buy more storage instead. The seen SharePoint card (2026-08-13) covered permissions, sharing links and retention, not storage.

**AI operator:** It runs version-storage reports, explains them site by site, then applies limits and queues trim jobs inside guardrails the admin states in plain English. It tracks job progress and reports the space reclaimed.

**Killer example:** 'We're 2 TB over quota. Free it up without touching Legal or anything from the last 90 days.' The AI ranks sites by version storage and excludes the Legal hub. It sets automatic version limits on the top 40 sites and queues `New-SPOSiteFileVersionBatchDeleteJob -DeleteBeforeDays 90` for each. It then reports 2.6 TB reclaimable and tracks the jobs to completion.

**Integration path:** SharePoint Online PowerShell `New-SPOSiteFileVersionBatchDeleteJob` (`-DeleteBeforeDays`, `-MajorVersionLimit`, `-Automatic`), `Get-SPOSiteFileVersionBatchDeleteJobProgress`, version-expiration report jobs, and tenant properties `EnableAutoExpirationVersionTrim` / `MajorVersionLimit` / `ExpireVersionsAfterDays` (learn.microsoft.com/sharepoint/trim-versions, verified). Site- and library-level trims are documented with no preview notice; the tenant-wide trim job is still private preview, so the MVP works site by site.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 5. Meta Lead Ads Instant Forms (qualifying forms, per-market variants, CRM routing) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Instant Forms are the main lead source on Facebook and Instagram for local services, real estate, education and insurance, with pre-filled fields and no landing page.

**Why people bounce off it:** Most advertisers ship the default name/email/phone form and drown in junk leads. Higher-intent forms, qualifying multiple-choice questions, per-region or per-language clones, and routing each lead to the right CRM owner are fiddly to set up. The seen Meta cards covered campaign management (07-31) and the Conversions API (09-25), not lead intake.

**AI operator:** The advertiser describes the ideal lead. The AI builds a qualifying form with custom questions and a higher-intent setting, clones it per market, subscribes the Page to lead webhooks and routes each lead into the CRM by its answers.

**Killer example:** 'Make a roofing-quote form that asks whether they own the home, their zip code and the roof's age, and send Austin leads to Mike and Dallas leads to Priya.' The AI creates the form with three qualifying questions and subscribes the Page to `leadgen` webhooks. It then writes a routing rule that sets the HubSpot owner by zip code. A week later it reports that 30% of leads answered 'renting' and suggests ending those forms with a polite disqualify message.

**Integration path:** Graph API `POST /{page_id}/leadgen_forms` (questions array with custom question types and options), `POST /{page_id}/subscribed_apps` for `leadgen`, `GET /{lead_id}`. The reference also lists `conditional_questions_group_id` and `dependent_conditional_questions`, but these are undocumented and one forum report shows a capability error, so branching questions are a stretch goal, not the MVP.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 6. Azure Virtual Desktop + Windows 365 (host pools, scaling plans, Cloud PC provisioning policies) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Microsoft's VDI and Cloud PC platform, used by a large share of mid-market and enterprise IT for call centres, contractors and remote staff.

**Why people bounce off it:** It is full of easy-to-miss settings:
- host pool types and session limits
- scaling-plan schedules that must not overlap
- the Power On/Off role, which must sit at subscription scope
- drain-mode exclusions and FSLogix profiles
- on the Windows 365 side, provisioning policies, images and network connections

Small IT teams misconfigure it and pay for VMs that run all night. Copilot in Intune for Windows 365 only gives insights such as resize candidates and unused licenses. It doesn't create anything.

**AI operator:** It builds and fixes host pools, scaling plans and Cloud PC policies from a plain description of who works when. It shows the cost and capacity effect before it applies anything.

**Killer example:** 'Pooled desktops for 80 call-centre agents, 8am to 6pm IST on weekdays, everything off at night, with Teams optimized.' The AI creates the host pool, desktop app group and workspace and sets the session limit. It creates a scaling plan with ramp-up, peak, ramp-down and off-peak phases and assigns the Power On/Off role at subscription scope. It then reports projected VM-hours against the current always-on baseline.

**Integration path:** ARM `Microsoft.DesktopVirtualization` resources: `hostPools`, `applicationGroups`, `workspaces`, and `scalingPlans` via `PUT .../scalingPlans/{name}?api-version=2025-10-10` (verified). Windows 365 uses Graph `POST /deviceManagement/virtualEndpoint/provisioningPolicies` plus `/assign`.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 7. DaVinci Resolve Fusion page (node-based titles and motion graphics) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Every free Resolve user gets a full node compositor for titles, tracking, keying and particles. That is an After Effects-class tool most of them never open.

**Why people bounce off it:** Editors think in layers, and Fusion is node graphs. Even a simple animated lower-third needs Background → Text+ → Merge → Transform plus keyframes. The seen Resolve card was about the edit and timeline side, and the Nuke card targeted pro VFX compositors. Neither served the YouTuber who just wants a branded lower-third.

**AI operator:** The user describes the graphic. The AI builds the node tree, wires it, keyframes it and saves it as a reusable Fusion macro or Edit-page title template.

**Killer example:** 'A lower-third that slides in from the left on a blurred backing bar in my brand orange, holds for 4 seconds, then fades out.' The AI adds Background, Text+, Blur, Merge and Transform tools and connects their inputs. It sets the slide and fade keyframes, then saves a `.setting` macro that shows up as a draggable title on the Edit page.

**Integration path:** FusionScript (Lua/Python) through Resolve's external scripting API: `comp:AddTool`, `SetInput`, `ConnectInput`, keyframe splines. `.comp` and `.setting` files are plain Lua tables, so a generator can also write them directly (the `pysion` library does this). Blackmagic ships no AI for Fusion authoring.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 8. Microsoft 365 license optimizer (group-based licensing, service plans, inactive premium seats) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Licensing decides what every user can do, and it is usually the biggest recurring Microsoft line item. Group-based licensing with disabled service plans can encode policy precisely.

**Why people bounce off it:** The admin portal shows cryptic SKU and service-plan names (ENTERPRISEPACK, disabledPlans GUIDs). Group-licensing conflicts are hard to untangle. Finding inactive E5 or Copilot seats means cross-checking usage reports by hand. The seen Google Workspace Admin card (2026-08-13) mentioned licence reclamation for Google only.

**AI operator:** It joins license holdings with activity reports and proposes downgrades and reassignments in plain English with the monthly saving. It applies them through group licensing with a rollback list.

**Killer example:** 'Find everyone on E5 who hasn't used Teams Phone or Defender in 90 days and move them to E3, but leave Finance alone.' The AI builds the diff (47 users, $1,880/month). It moves them to an E3 license group with the right `disabledPlans` and removes their direct E5 assignments. It then exports a rollback CSV.

**Integration path:** Graph `POST /users/{id}/assignLicense` and `POST /groups/{id}/assignLicense` with `disabledPlans`, `GET /subscribedSkus`, usage reports (`getOffice365ActiveUserDetail`, Teams and Copilot usage). Gap check: Microsoft's GA Admin agent can assign a license when asked and confirms every write. Its documented recommendations only cover Copilot-license targeting, not SKU downgrades across the tenant. Still, it is the nearest first-party overlap on today's list, so watch it.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 9. Excel CUBE formulas over Power BI semantic models (CUBEVALUE / CUBEMEMBER / CUBESET report grids) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** It gives finance a free-form board-pack or P&L layout in Excel where every cell is a live, governed number from the company's Power BI model, refreshed with one click.

**Why people bounce off it:** CUBE formulas need exact MDX member unique names like `[Date].[Fiscal Year].&[2026]` and nested CUBESET/CUBERANKEDMEMBER calls. Nobody writes them by hand. People convert a PivotTable to formulas, which breaks the first time the model changes, or they paste static values. An Aug 2026 Chris Webb post found Copilot in Excel pulls a static DAX extract rather than building connected formulas. The 2026-09-22 SSAS card was about building the model, not consuming it in Excel.

**AI operator:** It reads the model's dimensions and members and writes a live CUBE-formula report grid into the sheet. When the model changes it repairs broken member references.

**Killer example:** 'Build a monthly P&L by region for FY26 against budget from the Finance model, months across and my account rollup down.' The AI looks up the account-hierarchy members and writes CUBEMEMBER header rows and columns. It fills the body with CUBEVALUE formulas tied to a region slicer cell, then flags 3 accounts that have no budget rows.

**Integration path:** Power BI REST `POST /groups/{ws}/datasets/{id}/executeQueries` (DAX) to discover members. Office.js `Range.formulas` writes the grid. The workbook's model connection comes from one 'Insert PivotTable from Power BI' click; generating the connection in the OOXML package is unverified, hence buildability 3.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 10. Blackmagic ATEM switchers (SuperSource, keyers, DVE, macros) `closed (OSS control library)`

**Why it's powerful:** The ATEM Mini line is the default live-production switcher for churches, schools, streamers and small events, and it has broadcast-grade keying and picture-in-picture.

**Why people bounce off it:** Volunteers can cut between cameras, but they can't build a SuperSource layout, a chroma or luma key, or a macro. Macros have to be recorded click by click in perfect order, and there is no macro editor. The seen OBS and vMix cards are software switchers; ATEM is the hardware most of these rooms actually own.

**AI operator:** The user describes a look or sequence in plain English. The AI sets the switcher state and records it as a named macro, ready for a Stream Deck or Companion button.

**Killer example:** 'Make a two-person interview layout, pastor on the left and slides on the right with a 5% border, plus a macro that fades to it with the lower-third keyed on.' The AI sets the SuperSource box sources, positions and crops and configures the upstream DVE keyer. It records the transition sequence into macro slot 3, named 'Interview'.

**Integration path:** The open-source `atem-connection` TypeScript library (Sofie/NRK) speaks the switcher's LAN protocol and has setters for SuperSource boxes, keyers, DVE and macro recording. ATEM Software Control's XML export/import (which includes the macro pool) is a second path. Only a hobby MCP exists; Blackmagic ships no AI. Demand is scored 3 because the buyer is a volunteer tech team, not an enterprise.

**Demand 3/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 11. Zendesk data model: conditional ticket forms, custom objects, lookup relationships `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Custom objects with lookup fields turn Zendesk into a light CRM or asset tracker. Conditional ticket forms decide exactly which fields agents and end users see and must fill in.

**Why people bounce off it:** Admins have to manage condition sets per form and per audience (agent vs end user), 'required when' rules, plan-tiered lookup limits and object keys. One API trap makes it worse: sending an empty conditions array silently deletes every condition. The seen Zendesk card (2026-08-03) covered triggers, macros, SLAs and routing, not the schema.

**AI operator:** It designs the schema from a description: custom objects, lookup fields, ticket fields and forms with agent and end-user conditions. It diffs every change against the current config and guards against the empty-array wipe.

**Killer example:** 'Track customer devices. RMA tickets must pick a device owned by the requester's organization, and the serial number is required only when the issue type is Hardware.' The AI creates a `device` custom object with an organization lookup, adds a filtered device lookup field on tickets and builds an RMA form. It adds a condition that makes Serial required when Issue Type is Hardware.

**Integration path:** `POST /api/v2/ticket_forms` with writable `agent_conditions` and `end_user_conditions` (docs warn about the empty-array wipe). Custom Objects and Custom Object Fields APIs; lookup fields use `type: "lookup"` with targets like `zen:custom_object:KEY` (verified by the scout in Zendesk docs). Zendesk's Copilot is agent-facing; it doesn't design schemas.

**Demand 3/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 12. Katana MRP variant BOMs and operation routings `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** A cloud MRP for small makers that sits on top of QuickBooks, Xero and Shopify. It tracks live stock, runs manufacturing orders and costs BOMs per unit.

**Why people bounce off it:** BOMs and operations are set per variant. A product with 40 variants needs 40 recipes plus 40 operation routings, entered row by row. Accurate costing also needs labour and overhead rates on every step. The 2026-10-19 Fishbowl card was about reconciling inventory with QuickBooks; this card is about the setup work that comes before any of that.

**AI operator:** The maker describes the product family in plain English. The AI builds the variant matrix, recipe rows per variant and operation rows with times and rates. It shows the cost per unit for every variant before writing anything.

**Killer example:** 'Our candles come in 3 sizes and 8 scents. Large uses twice the wax and 1.5 times the fragrance oil. Pouring takes 10 minutes and curing takes 48 hours.' The AI creates 24 variant recipes and their operation rows, then prints a unit-cost table and flags the 2 scents whose margin is under 40% at current prices.

**Integration path:** Katana REST `POST /v1/recipes` (bulk, 150 rows per call), `POST /v1/product_operation_rows` (bulk) and the products/variants endpoints (developer.katanamrp.com, verified by the scout). Unleashed is a natural second connector.

**Demand 3/5 · Buildability 4/5**
