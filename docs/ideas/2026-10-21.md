### 1. Jira admin schemes: workflows, workflow schemes, screens, screen schemes, permission schemes ("scheme hell") `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Every Jira project can carry its own workflow, workflow scheme, screen scheme, issue-type screen scheme and permission scheme. That is what lets one Jira site model legal intake, hardware RMA and software delivery side by side across thousands of projects.

**Why people bounce off it:** Five interlocking scheme types, each shared across many projects. Changing one transition can silently change 40 projects that share the scheme, and switching schemes forces a status migration for every existing issue. Whole "Jira admin" jobs exist only to keep this straight. The 2026-07-31 Jira card covered JQL and Automation rules; nobody has pitched the scheme layer.

**AI operator:** A plain-language Jira admin. It maps which projects share each scheme before touching anything, clones instead of editing shared schemes, runs Atlassian's own validation endpoints before every write, generates the old-to-new status mappings for existing issues, and shows a blast-radius diff ("affects 1 project, 1,200 issues") for approval.

**Killer example:** A Legal ops lead says: 'Make our contracts project work like Finance's, but add a Legal Approval step before Signed that only legal-ops can move.' The AI clones Finance's workflow, adds the status and a group-restricted transition, validates it, builds a new workflow scheme for the Legal project only, maps the statuses of 1,200 existing issues, and grants legal-ops the transition permission. Finance is untouched.

**Integration path:** Jira Cloud REST v3, confirmed in Atlassian's official OpenAPI spec: `POST /rest/api/3/workflows/create` and `/workflows/update` plus their `/validation` variants; `POST /rest/api/3/workflowscheme`, `/workflowscheme/update`, `/workflowscheme/update/mappings`, `/workflowscheme/project/switch`; `POST /rest/api/3/screens`, `/screens/{id}/tabs/{tabId}/fields`, `/screenscheme`, `/issuetypescreenscheme`; `POST /rest/api/3/permissionscheme` and `/permissionscheme/{id}/permission`. Field configuration endpoints are marked deprecated in the spec (Atlassian is moving to field schemes), so leave them out of the MVP. Rovo generates automation rules and work items, not schemes.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 2. Exchange Online + Outlook delegation and resource booking: shared mailboxes, SendAs/FullAccess, calendar delegates, room and equipment mailboxes, Set-CalendarProcessing `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Every Microsoft 365 tenant runs shared mailboxes, executive-assistant delegation, room and equipment mailboxes and booking policies on Exchange Online.

**Why people bounce off it:** Several overlapping permission types that everyone confuses: FullAccess, SendAs, SendOnBehalf, folder-level Editor and Delegate, and calendar sharing flags. Room booking is controlled by `Set-CalendarProcessing`, which has about 40 parameters (AutoAccept, BookInPolicy, RequestInPolicy, ResourceDelegates, MaximumDurationInMinutes, conflict and recurrence rules), and most of it isn't in the admin center GUI. "Why did room 4B decline my meeting?" is a ticket admins can't answer quickly. The earlier Exchange card covered mail flow only, and the Outlook/Gmail cards covered inbox rules.

**AI operator:** Plain-English policy in, cmdlets out. It snapshots the current permissions and booking policy, shows the resulting permission map as a diff, applies it, and explains why any given meeting request was accepted, declined or sent for approval.

**Killer example:** An IT admin says: 'Board room: only the exec team books directly, everyone else needs Priya's approval, max 4 hours, no recurring meetings past 6 months. And give Anil send-as on finance@ but not inbox access.' The AI sets BookInPolicy, RequestInPolicy, ResourceDelegates, MaximumDurationInMinutes and the recurrence window, runs `Add-RecipientPermission -AccessRights SendAs` for Anil, skips FullAccess, and shows the before/after map.

**Integration path:** Exchange Online PowerShell with certificate auth: `New-Mailbox -Room`, `Set-CalendarProcessing`, `Add-MailboxPermission`, `Add-RecipientPermission`, `Add-MailboxFolderPermission -SharingPermissionFlags Delegate`, `Set-Place`; Graph Places API for room metadata. Google Workspace connector later via Admin SDK `resources.calendars.insert` and Calendar API `acl.insert`.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 3. Printify (print-on-demand catalog fan-out), with Printful and Gelato as second connectors `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Millions of registered merchants. 1,000+ blueprints, several print providers per blueprint with different base costs, shipping and regions, and one-click publishing to Etsy, Shopify, eBay and TikTok Shop.

**Why people bounce off it:** For every product the seller has to pick a provider, compare variant costs and shipping, set a price per variant across every size and colour by hand, position the artwork on each print area, then publish with tags. 50 designs times 5 product types is days of clicking. Printify's own AI only generates images and mockups.

**AI operator:** Catalog fan-out. Give it one design and a margin target; it picks the provider by cost, region and rating, prices every variant to the target margin after shipping, places the artwork, and publishes.

**Killer example:** An Etsy seller says: 'Put my Sunset Cat art on a tee, hoodie and mug, cheapest US provider, keep 35% margin after shipping, publish to Etsy.' The AI uploads the image, compares providers on variant and shipping cost, creates three products with per-variant prices and print-area placement, and publishes them.

**Integration path:** Printify REST v1 with a personal access token: `POST /v1/uploads/images.json`; `GET /v1/catalog/blueprints/{id}/print_providers.json` and the per-provider `variants.json` and `shipping.json`; `POST /v1/shops/{shop_id}/products.json` (blueprint, provider, per-variant price, print-area placeholders with x, y, scale, angle); `POST /v1/shops/{shop_id}/products/{id}/publish.json`. Printful store products and Gelato's create-from-template as later connectors.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 4. Amazon Seller Central: Send to Amazon / FBA inbound shipments `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** It is the only way more than a million FBA sellers get stock into Amazon's warehouses: multi-destination placement, carton packing groups, partnered-carrier SPD/LTL quotes, delivery windows, and FBA and carton labels.

**Why people bounce off it:** A 4-6 step wizard where each step generates options you then confirm: packing, then placement, then transport, then delivery window. "Minimal splits" quietly adds placement fees, box contents are entered per carton, fees only appear late in the flow, and a mistake usually means cancelling and starting over. The 2026-08-06 Seller Central card covered listings repair and ads, not inbound.

**AI operator:** A shipment planner. It builds the plan from a plain description of how the boxes are packed, generates every placement and transport option, prices each combination (placement fee plus freight), and confirms the cheapest after approval.

**Killer example:** A seller says: 'Send 480 of the blue mugs, 24 per box, from my Ohio warehouse, cheapest overall.' The AI creates the inbound plan, prices every option and replies '2 fulfillment centres with SPD: $312 total, vs $389 for a single FC.' On approval it confirms and returns the label PDFs.

**Integration path:** SP-API Fulfillment Inbound v2024-03-20, which covers the whole workflow: `createInboundPlan`, `generatePackingOptions` / `confirmPackingOption`, `setPackingInformation`, `generatePlacementOptions` / `confirmPlacementOption`, `generateTransportationOptions` / `confirmTransportationOptions`, delivery-window confirmation, then labels. LWA app registration and async operation polling. Amazon's agentic Seller Assistant can recommend shipments but there is no evidence it builds and confirms them.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 5. Salesforce Collaborative Forecasts, Quotas and Opportunity Splits `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Multiple forecast types (revenue, quantity, product family, splits, territory) rolled up the role or territory hierarchy, with manager adjustments, custom forecast categories and quota attainment.

**Why people bounce off it:** Setup is a chain of linked config: forecast types, hierarchy, fiscal periods, split types and measure/date fields. Worse, quotas can't be entered in Setup at all; admins load ForecastingQuota rows per user, per month, per forecast type with Data Loader. "My forecast shows 0% attainment" is the classic result. None of the earlier Salesforce cards covered forecasting.

**AI operator:** A forecast setup assistant. It configures forecast types, turns a quota spreadsheet or a sentence into ForecastingQuota rows, sets up split types, and checks the rollups each manager will see.

**Killer example:** A sales-ops manager says: 'Q3 quotas: Sarah's team gets $1.2M split evenly, Raj gets 20% more than last quarter, and overlay reps get 50% credit on splits.' The AI creates an overlay split type at 50% and the matching forecast type, computes per-rep monthly figures, upserts the quota rows, and shows the attainment view per manager.

**Integration path:** Metadata API `ForecastingSettings` deployed with `sf project deploy`; REST / Bulk 2.0 create and upsert on `ForecastingQuota` (needs Manage Quotas); REST on `OpportunitySplitType` and `OpportunitySplit`; read `ForecastingItem` to verify. Agentforce Forecast Guidance only reads forecasts.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 6. Employment Hero Payroll (formerly KeyPay): award and enterprise-agreement pay condition rule sets `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Payroll with built-in Australian Modern Award interpretation (penalty rates, overtime, allowances, leave accrual) for a very large AU/NZ/UK/SG/MY SME base.

**Why people bounce off it:** Pay condition rule sets. As soon as an enterprise agreement or a split-award employee departs from the stock award, the admin has to unlock the award and hand-edit rules and pay rate templates. Custom rule sets stop receiving automatic award updates, so mistakes quietly cause underpayment, which in Australia is a legal problem.

**AI operator:** Reads the agreement clause in plain English, clones the nearest stock rule set, edits it, and replays recent timesheets to show the pay difference before saving.

**Killer example:** A payroll officer says: 'Our EA pays double time after 10 hours on Saturdays, not after 2. Apply it to warehouse staff only.' The AI clones the warehouse rule set, changes the Saturday overtime threshold, re-points the warehouse employees, and shows that 14 recent shifts change by $1,870 in total.

**Integration path:** Employment Hero Payroll API "Pay Condition" endpoints, released 11 Feb 2026 per Employment Hero's partner product-update page: list, create, update, delete and clone of rule sets per business (paths in the regional API Reference on api.keypay.com.au). Pay category and employee endpoints cover the rest. Hero AI flags pay anomalies but doesn't edit rule sets.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 7. MuseScore Studio (music notation), with Sibelius and Dorico as later connectors `OSS`

**Why it's powerful:** The most-used free notation app, with millions of installs and the musescore.com sharing site. Full engraving, instrument parts, Muse Sounds playback and MusicXML.

**Why people bounce off it:** Note entry means memorising modes and shortcuts for voices, tuplets, grace notes and cross-staff notation. Layout lives in Format > Style. Generating parts, transposing and fixing collisions stop hobbyists and school music teachers.

**AI operator:** An "Engraver" that turns plain musical intent into score edits, then runs a layout pass.

**Killer example:** A teacher says: 'Arrange this melody for string quartet, give the cello a walking bass, then make each part fit on two pages.' The AI adds the staves, writes the voices, generates the parts and tightens staff spacing and measures per system.

**Integration path:** MuseScore plugin API (QML/JS): `curScore.startCmd()`, `newCursor()`, `cursor.setDuration()`, `cursor.addNote()`, `curScore.appendPart()` and element properties. Headless route: write `.mscx` inside the `.mscz` zip or MusicXML, render with `mscore -o`. Later: Sibelius ManuScript plugins. Dorico's Remote Control API only triggers commands. A community MuseScore MCP exists, so polish and teaching UX are the moat.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 8. OriginLab Origin / OriginPro (scientific graphing and curve fitting) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** The standard graphing and analysis tool in physics, chemistry, materials and engineering labs: publication-grade multi-layer graphs, 100+ built-in nonlinear fit functions, peak analysis, signal processing and batch analysis templates.

**Why people bounce off it:** Graphs are layers, plots and axis objects whose properties sit several dialog tabs deep: tick formats, axis breaks, stacked panels, shared axes, journal styles. Nonlinear fits need the right function, initial parameters, constraints and shared parameters. Researchers spend hours reproducing a figure style they saw in a paper.

**AI operator:** A "Figure Butler" that reads the open project's workbooks and graphs, builds or restyles graphs, runs fits and analysis templates, and applies journal presets from plain language.

**Killer example:** A PhD student says: 'Stack these three XRD scans as offset panels with one shared 2-theta axis, label peaks above 500 counts, ACS single-column style, export a 600-dpi TIFF.' The AI builds the multi-panel graph, offsets the layers, runs peak finding, labels the peaks, applies the style and exports.

**Integration path:** The `originpro` Python package over Origin's Automation Server (COM): `op.new_sheet`, `wks.from_df`, `op.new_graph(template=...)`, `gl.add_plot`, `layer.set_xlim`; the `op.NLFit` class for fitting; `op.lt_exec` for any LabTalk property; `.otpu` graph templates and `.oth` themes for saved styles. Windows only and needs an Origin licence. No first-party AI agent found.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 9. SAP Analysis for Microsoft Office (Excel add-in over BW/BW4HANA/S/4), with Oracle Smart View as second connector `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Live multi-dimensional access to SAP BW and S/4HANA inside Excel: crosstabs, hierarchies, variables and planning write-back. Most SAP-run finance teams have it installed.

**Why people bounce off it:** The Design Panel: dragging dimensions between rows and columns, choosing members in filter dialogs, filling variable prompts, hand-writing `SAPGetData` and `SAPListOf` formulas, and running planning sequences to write plan data back. Most analysts paste a static export and lose the live link.

**AI operator:** Describe the analysis; the agent sets variables, filters and layout, refreshes, writes the reconciling formulas, and runs planning functions with a changed-cells preview before saving.

**Killer example:** An FP&A analyst says: 'Show Q3 actual vs plan by profit center, EMEA only, months across the top, and copy plan into forecast for October.' The AI sets the fiscal-period variable, filters EMEA, moves calendar month to columns, runs the copy planning function, and shows the changed cells before saving.

**Integration path:** Analysis for Office VBA API through `Application.Run`: `SAPSetVariable`, `SAPSetFilter`, `SAPMoveDimension`, `SAPExecuteCommand`, `SAPExecutePlanningFunction`, `SAPExecutePlanningSequence`, `SAPSetData`, plus `SAPGetData` formulas. Smart View: `HypConnect`, `HypSetPOV`, `HypRetrieve`, `HypPivot`, `HypSubmitData`. Ships as a VSTO/COM add-in. Gotcha: most API calls are blocked inside the AfterRedisplay callback (SAP KBAs 2350649, 2577920). Joule has nothing for Analysis for Office grids.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 10. Jira Plans (formerly Advanced Roadmaps) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** One plan pulls work from many projects and boards, with hierarchy above epics, team capacity, dependencies, cross-project releases, scenarios and auto-scheduling. It is the portfolio tool on Jira Premium and Enterprise.

**Why people bounce off it:** Configuring issue sources, exclusion rules, plan-only teams with velocity and sprint length, and cross-project releases, then trying to understand why auto-schedule moved your dates. Most PMs export to Excel instead. Atlassian's Rovo MCP doesn't support Plans yet (feature request CRUC-8709).

**AI operator:** A conversational re-planner. It reads the plan, models capacity and dependencies itself (the API doesn't run auto-schedule), and writes target dates, teams and dependency links back to live issues behind a preview diff.

**Killer example:** A PM says: 'Mobile team loses two engineers in January. Re-sequence so Payments v2 still ships by March 31 and tell me what slips.' The AI lowers the team's capacity, recomputes the schedule, writes target start/end on 37 issues, adds two blocks links, and lists the four epics that now miss the quarter.

**Integration path:** Plans endpoints in Atlassian's official REST v3 spec, all flagged experimental: `POST /rest/api/3/plans/plan`, `PUT /rest/api/3/plans/plan/{planId}` (issue sources, scheduling, exclusion rules, cross-project releases), `.../duplicate`, `.../archive`, `POST .../team/planonly` and `.../team/atlassian`. Issue writes via `PUT /rest/api/3/issue/{key}` and `POST /rest/api/3/issueLink`. Unsaved scenarios can't be written, so the operator commits to live issues after approval.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 11. Akamai Property Manager (rule trees, versions, staging/production activation) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** A large share of enterprise CDN traffic runs on it. A property's rule tree controls caching, origin routing, redirects, headers, edge auth and image handling across Akamai's edge.

**Why people bounce off it:** Nested rules with match criteria and all/any logic where later rules silently override earlier ones, hundreds of behaviours named for CDN experts, and a version / staging / production activation cycle. Teams that inherit a property often don't know what it does, and the usual fix is an Akamai professional-services engagement. No first-party AI assistant in Control Center.

**AI operator:** A rule-tree navigator. It explains what happens to any URL (which rules match, in what order, final behaviours), writes changes as a diff on a cloned version, activates to staging, tests through the staging edge, then promotes, with Fast Fallback as rollback.

**Killer example:** A platform engineer says: 'Stop caching /api/cart but keep 1-day caching on /api/catalog, and redirect old /shop/* links to /store/*.' The AI clones v42 to v43, inserts a path-match rule above the catch-all cache rule, adds the redirect, activates on staging, curls both paths through the staging edge to show cache headers, and asks before activating on production.

**Integration path:** Property Manager API (PAPI): `GET`/`PUT /papi/v1/properties/{propertyId}/versions/{v}/rules` (validation errors returned inline), `POST /papi/v1/properties/{propertyId}/versions` to clone, `POST /papi/v1/properties/{propertyId}/activations` with `useFastFallback`. AppSec API for security configs later; Akamai CLI and Terraform provider as fallbacks.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 12. Microsoft Configuration Manager (MECM / SCCM): collections, deployments, maintenance windows, software update groups `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** It still runs patching, app deployment and OS imaging for most large on-prem or co-managed Windows estates. It is distinct from Intune.

**Why people bounce off it:** Collections defined by WQL query rules with limiting collections and include/exclude rules; deployments with purpose, deadlines, maintenance windows and distribution points; software update groups and Automatic Deployment Rules; task sequences on top. The console is decades of accreted UI learned by apprenticeship, and Microsoft's Copilot work targets Intune, not MECM.

**AI operator:** A patch-and-deploy co-admin. It builds collections from plain targeting, shows the matching device count before writing, creates deployments and maintenance windows, and explains why a given machine did or didn't get something.

**Killer example:** An admin says: 'Push Chrome 131 to all Finance laptops, not servers, required by Friday 6pm, only in the 8pm-4am window.' The AI writes a WQL collection (Finance OU, laptop chassis, not a server OS) limited to All Workstations, reports '214 devices match', adds the maintenance window, and creates a required deployment with a Friday 18:00 deadline.

**Integration path:** ConfigurationManager PowerShell module: `New-CMDeviceCollection`, `Add-CMDeviceCollectionQueryMembershipRule`, `New-CMMaintenanceWindow`, `New-CMApplicationDeployment`, `New-CMSoftwareUpdateGroup`, `New-CMSoftwareUpdateDeployment`, `New-CMSoftwareUpdateAutoDeploymentRule`. AdminService REST (`/AdminService/wmi/SMS_Collection`) for reads and collections. Needs an on-prem agent with Windows auth.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**
