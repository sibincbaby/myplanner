### 1. Camelot / pdfplumber (open-source PDF table extraction; Tabula as GUI sibling) feeding Excel / Google Sheets `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** Tuned correctly, Camelot and pdfplumber extract accurate tables from almost any PDF, headless, into DataFrames. Nearly every office worker needs 'numbers out of this PDF into Excel'.

**Why people bounce off it:** Parameters are opaque and differ per layout (lattice vs stream, table_areas, columns, row_tol, edge_tol, snap tolerances). Multi-page tables with repeated headers, merged cells, footers in the body, parenthesised negatives and scanned PDFs all break defaults. People retype or pay for SaaS.

**AI operator:** User drops PDFs and says which table and what the columns mean. The operator inspects the page, picks lattice or stream, sets areas and column separators, extracts, then self-verifies by reconciling to document totals, per-page row counts and column type consistency. Cleans up in plain language, writes .xlsx or a Sheets tab, and saves the tuned parameters as a reusable per-vendor template so next month runs unattended. OCR for scans.

**Killer example:** User: 'Here are 14 monthly supplier statements. One sheet: Date, Invoice #, Amount (credit notes negative), and flag any month where the lines don't sum to the Statement Total.' AI tries lattice on page 1, finds no ruling lines, switches to stream with column x-positions, strips repeated headers on pages 2-3, converts '(450.00)' to -450.00, and merges all months. Month 9 is off by 120.00: it shows the page where a wrapped description swallowed an amount, fixes that file's column split, and re-checks. Final workbook has a Reconciled yes/no column per statement, plus a saved template per supplier layout so next month's batch runs with zero clicks.

**Integration path:** Python libraries and CLIs (Camelot, pdfplumber) returning DataFrames/CSV; openpyxl/pandas for .xlsx or Google Sheets API; ocrmypdf/Tesseract for scans. Reconcile step is plain code. Solo MVP is a Python service plus chat UI; per-vendor templates are the moat. Stirling-PDF, Paperless and Power Query's PDF connector are covered elsewhere but none do tuned, reconciled multi-page extraction.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 2. EN 16931 e-invoicing formats: Factur-X/ZUGFeRD, XRechnung and Peppol BIS Billing 3 (France 2026 reform, Germany phase-in, Belgium Peppol), across ERPs and accounting tools `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** EN 16931 is the single semantic model behind EU B2B e-invoicing mandates, so one correct invoice can pass through PDP/Peppol/portal routes in several countries. The tooling is open: Mustang Project, KoSIT validator, Schematron rule sets, Peppol open-source access-point stacks.

**Why people bounce off it:** Invoices bounce with opaque rule IDs (BR-CO-15, BR-DE-1, PEPPOL-EN16931-R003) tied to BT-xx business terms. Fixing needs VAT category codes, exemption reasons, buyer-reference rules, endpoint and scheme IDs and profile knowledge. ERPs export half-valid XML and accountants see only 'rejected by platform' plus a Schematron dump. Rules differ by country and CIUS.

**AI operator:** A compliance operator pre-loaded with the rule catalogue, country CIUS rules and code lists. It validates outgoing and incoming invoices (XML or PDF/A-3) with real validators, explains each failure in business language, and proposes the minimal fix to the source record (customer master, tax code, payment means), not just the XML. It generates compliant Factur-X/XRechnung from PDFs or CSV batches, maps ERP tax codes to EN 16931 VAT categories once, and keeps a per-customer routing table. Every fix is an approved diff.

**Killer example:** User: 'Our Munich customer's portal rejected 14 invoices last week, and the French subsidiary needs the same fixed before Friday.' AI validates all 14 with the KoSIT validator and collapses them into 3 root causes: missing BT-10 buyer reference, VAT category E with no exemption reason, IBAN under the wrong payment-means code. It patches the customer master and tax-code mapping in the ERP once, regenerates all XML, re-validates to green, and queues resubmission with a one-line summary per invoice. It then applies the same mapping to the French subsidiary's batch and flags 2 invoices needing a human decision.

**Integration path:** Headless and open: Java/Python validators (KoSIT, Mustang, ph-schematron) and Factur-X libraries in a container. Source side: Odoo/ERPNext/Dolibarr/Akaunting APIs, Xero and QuickBooks REST, or plain PDF/CSV upload. Send side: Peppol access-point or PDP sandbox APIs. A solo dev can ship validate-explain-fix-regenerate on uploaded files in weeks; live ERP write-back is phase 2.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 3. ClickHouse (open-source columnar OLAP DB; self-hosted, ClickHouse Cloud, Altinity) - schema and query tuning operator `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** Extremely fast columnar analytics if the table design is right, with rich system tables (query_log, parts, columns) exposing exactly what is slow.

**Why people bounce off it:** ORDER BY, partition key, codecs, LowCardinality, skip indexes, projections, MV chains and TTL must be decided up front and a wrong choice means a full rewrite. Failures like 'Too many parts', Memory limit exceeded, 100% granule scans and silently broken MVs need expert reading of system.parts, query_log and EXPLAIN indexes=1.

**AI operator:** Reads system.query_log, system.parts, system.columns and EXPLAIN PLAN indexes=1, finds the costliest query shapes, and proposes concrete DDL (new ORDER BY, projection, skip index, codec, aggregating MV). Tests each candidate on a sampled copy, reports measured granules and bytes before and after, and emits a zero-downtime backfill and EXCHANGE TABLES swap script.

**Killer example:** User: 'Our events dashboard query takes 40 seconds and ingestion keeps hitting Too many parts.' AI finds the table partitioned by toDate(ts) plus a user_id hash (about 9k partitions) with ORDER BY (ts), while 90% of queries filter on tenant_id + event_type. It proposes PARTITION BY toYYYYMM(ts), ORDER BY (tenant_id, event_type, toStartOfHour(ts)), LowCardinality(String) for event_type and async_insert. It benchmarks the top 5 real queries on a 1% copy (granules read drop from 98% to 3%, 40s to 0.9s), writes monthly-chunk INSERT-SELECT backfill, and swaps atomically after approval.

**Integration path:** Plain SQL over HTTP/native interface, clickhouse-client, clickhouse-local; system tables as read-only telemetry. Free Docker/local and Cloud trial for testing. Read-only audit first, then DDL generation and copy-benchmarking as the paid step.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 4. Cilium + Hubble (eBPF CNI and network observability; CNCF graduated) - Zero-Trust Network Policy Writer for Kubernetes `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** Cilium is the default CNI on GKE Dataplane V2 and widely used elsewhere, and Hubble records the true flow graph. L3/L4/L7 and FQDN policy is expressive enough for real zero-trust.

**Why people bounce off it:** CiliumNetworkPolicy selector semantics, entity and FQDN rules, DNS visibility and default-deny side effects are unforgiving. One missing egress rule to CoreDNS or an external API breaks prod, so clusters stay flat and auditors object.

**AI operator:** Watches Hubble flows (hubble observe -o json / Relay gRPC) for a namespace over a chosen window, builds the real service graph, and generates least-privilege policies in audit mode first. Replays recorded flows against the proposed policy to predict drops, then stages enforcement namespace by namespace with automatic rollback if the DROPPED verdict rate spikes.

**Killer example:** User: 'Lock down the payments namespace so it only talks to what it needs.' AI observes 7 days of flows and finds payments to postgres:5432, kube-dns, api.stripe.com:443 (FQDN), with ingress only from checkout and gateway. It notices a rare monthly job to an S3 endpoint the window barely caught. It generates the policy set, runs audit mode for 48 hours, shows 0 unexpected drops and 2 would-drops (the S3 job and a Prometheus scrape), asks about those, adds the rules, enforces, and leaves a Grafana panel and a one-line rollback command.

**Integration path:** kubectl and CRDs, cilium/hubble CLIs, Hubble Relay gRPC and flow JSON, built-in policy audit mode. kind/minikube with Cilium is a free test bed. Read-only flow analysis plus policy generation is the MVP; GitOps PR path (ArgoCD/Flux) for production.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 5. RabbitMQ (management plugin + HTTP API + rabbitmqctl; also CloudAMQP/Amazon MQ) - Broker Doctor and the 4.x classic-mirroring exit `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** Ubiquitous message broker with a fully documented management API, definitions export/import, diagnostics CLI, Shovel and Federation, all scriptable.

**Why people bounce off it:** Behaviour is split across regex-matched policies (one wins per queue), operator policies, bindings, DLX/TTL args, queue types, prefetch and acks. Messages pile up Unacked, DLX silently drops, policies get shadowed. Classic mirrored queues were removed in 4.0 and queue args are immutable, so migration to quorum queues needs a careful plan.

**AI operator:** Pre-loaded with the management API, rabbitmq-diagnostics and rabbitmq-queues plus a policy-resolution simulator and definitions-diff engine. Explains why a queue behaves as it does, simulates which policy wins, and plans migrations as a dry-run diff of definitions.json before applying anything.

**Killer example:** User: 'The orders queue keeps growing and some messages vanish after a retry. Fix it and get us off mirrored queues before the 4.x upgrade.' AI finds 12k Unacked messages held by a consumer with prefetch=0, finds the 'orders.*' policy shadowed by a priority-10 'all-ha' policy so the DLX never attaches, and a retry loop dead-lettering into a consumerless queue. It produces a migration plan: declare orders.v2 as a quorum queue with delivery-limit and DLX, add a shovel to drain the old queue, flip the policy, and list the 7 apps whose code declares the old arguments. It applies policy and shovel changes after confirmation.

**Integration path:** REST management API (OpenAPI), definitions JSON, rabbitmqctl/diagnostics, Shovel/Federation, Prometheus plugin. Local Docker broker is a free test bed. Read-only first, then gated writes. MCP packaging is straightforward.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 6. Salesforce org metadata cleanup: unused fields, flows, Apex, reports and layouts (Optimizer successor job, via Tooling and Metadata APIs) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Every field, flow and layout is machine-readable through Metadata and Tooling APIs, and MetadataComponentDependency plus SOQL aggregates can prove a component is dead.

**Why people bounce off it:** Salesforce Optimizer is being retired and its free replacement Org Check is read-only lists with no safe removal plan. A field may be referenced by a flow, report filter, formula, Apex, integration or layout, and deleting breaks things silently, so orgs never clean up and accrue 800-field objects.

**AI operator:** Read-then-stage operator. Per candidate it gathers population %, last-modified, references in Flow XML, Apex, formulas, validation rules, FlexiPages, layouts, reports and dashboards, plus dependency data. Scores deletion risk and writes a staged deprecation plan: remove from layouts, then permission sets, rename with zzz_deprecated_, wait 30 days, then destructive deploy. Everything validates in sandbox or check-only deploy first.

**Killer example:** Admin: 'Find every custom field on Account and Opportunity under 2% populated and unused in any flow, report, formula or Apex, then stage them for retirement.' AI returns 143 candidates by risk: 89 safe, 41 referenced only by inactive flows, 13 blocked by a report or integration user. It generates layout and permission-set edits for the 89, a destructiveChanges.xml for a later wave, validates with a check-only deploy, and drafts the change note and 30-day calendar for the team.

**Integration path:** sf CLI plus REST, Tooling and Metadata APIs: SOQL aggregates, MetadataComponentDependency (beta, so also parse metadata itself), Metadata retrieve for Flow/Apex/Layout/FlexiPage, Analytics REST for reports, check-only deploy with destructiveChanges.xml. MVP is read-only audit plus generated deploy package in a few weeks. Frame as cleanup/deprecation, not deploy ordering.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 7. ThoughtSpot (Models, Liveboards, Sets/Formulas via TML) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Governed search over cloud warehouses with Liveboards, formulas, Sets, row-level security and embeds. Every object exports and imports as TML YAML through a documented REST API v2.

**Why people bounce off it:** The work that makes search useful (Models, joins, column properties, synonyms, formulas, Sets) lives in a dense admin UI. Garbage Models give wrong or empty answers and users stop trusting it. Dev/test/prod promotion means hand-moving TML.

**AI operator:** A Model Steward agent that reads warehouse schema and Model TML, writes TML with correct joins, types, aggregation defaults, synonyms mined from search logs and Slack/Jira vocabulary, and formulas. Validates with import_policy VALIDATE_ONLY, shows a diff, imports ALL_OR_NONE, handles dev-to-prod GUID/FQN rewriting and clones Liveboards per team.

**Killer example:** User: 'Sales keeps searching "ARR by segment" and gets nothing. Add ARR to the Revenue model as subscription MRR x 12 excluding churned, synonyms ARR and annual recurring, and give each region manager a Liveboard.' AI exports the Model TML, adds the formula column and synonyms, validates, imports, clones one Liveboard per region with the filter pre-applied, re-runs the failed searches from the logs to prove they now return answers, and posts the links.

**Integration path:** REST API v2 /metadata/tml/export and /import with import_policy and async status polling; TypeScript SDK; needs DATAMANAGEMENT or ADMINISTRATION privilege. Pitch as Model/Liveboard authoring, migration and promotion, not another chat-with-data box (Spotter exists).

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 8. Aha! Roadmaps (Productboard as second connector) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Strategy-to-release product management with scorecards, initiatives, goals, releases, ideas portals, Jira/ADO/GitHub integrations, a full REST API v1 and webhooks.

**Why people bounce off it:** Huge configuration surface (layouts, scorecards, statuses, report builders, integration mappings) that PMs rarely master. Quarterly review prep means hours of re-scoring, linking and fixing orphaned records, and rebuilding views per audience.

**AI operator:** A roadmap copilot that drives the REST API to triage ideas, dedupe and merge, score by a user-described formula (creating the custom scorecard fields), link ideas to features/initiatives, audit hygiene (features with no goal, releases with no dates, Jira mismatches), and generate audience-specific roadmap views and release-note drafts.

**Killer example:** User: 'Rescore everything in Q1 candidates by reach times impact over effort, merge duplicate SSO ideas, flag anything with no linked goal, and give me a one-page roadmap for sales that hides internal tech-debt.' AI creates the scorecard, scores 212 ideas, merges 14 SSO duplicates with a comment trail, lists 9 goal-less features for approval, and builds the filtered sales roadmap with a shareable link.

**Integration path:** Aha! REST API v1 (OAuth/API key, about 300 req/min) for features, ideas, initiatives, releases, custom fields, scorecards; webhooks; Productboard REST v2. Tool server with dry-run and diff approval. Layout and view creation is partly UI-only, so MVP focuses on records, scoring and hygiene.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 9. Jira Data Center to Cloud migration operator (Jira DC + JCMA + ScriptRunner/Groovy post-functions and listeners) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Jira DC estates hold a decade of workflows, listeners, Groovy post-functions, JQL dashboards and Marketplace apps. Atlassian is ending Data Center (around 2029) and stopped new DC subscriptions in 2026, so a large installed base must move.

**Why people bounce off it:** JCMA moves data but not behavior. ScriptRunner scripts, JMWE and DC automation have no 1:1 Cloud equivalent, nobody has a per-project dependency inventory, and Cloud enforces different limits. Admins learn what broke only after cutover.

**AI operator:** A migration-readiness agent reads the DC instance via REST, DB export or support zip and builds a dependency graph of workflows, post-functions, listeners, fields, filters and apps. Classifies each Groovy script (native Cloud feature, Automation rule, Forge/ScriptRunner Cloud rewrite, drop), drafts replacements and wave plans, and replays JQL and sample-transition tests on Cloud after each wave to report behavioral diffs.

**Killer example:** User: 'We have 140 projects and 60 ScriptRunner scripts on DC. Which projects can move this quarter with zero behavior loss, and what do the rest need?' AI scans and returns 'Wave 1: 52 projects with no script dependencies, ready now'. It rewrites the auto-assign-on-Review Groovy post-function as a Cloud automation rule, and gives the 9 projects whose scripts call internal DC Java APIs a Forge rewrite estimate and test plan.

**Integration path:** DC REST API and Cloud REST v3, workflow XML export, UPM app list, project/field config. Cloud automation rules and Forge apps via API/CLI. JCMA is UI-driven so the operator plans and verifies around it. Solo MVP: read-only audit, dependency graph and script classification report in weeks.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 10. Amazon Vendor Central (1P wholesale: Vendor Orders, Shipments, Invoices, Chargebacks, Retail Analytics) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** How brands sell wholesale to Amazon at large guaranteed PO volumes. SP-API covers Vendor Orders, Shipments, Invoices, Direct Fulfillment and vendor reports for sales, inventory and payments.

**Why people bounce off it:** Amazon deducts shortage, ASN-mismatch, late-ship and routing chargebacks, each needing matching to a PO, ASN and carrier proof of delivery before disputing in a clunky Case Log. Brands run on spreadsheets and a part-time contractor, so much chargeback money is never disputed.

**AI operator:** A vendor compliance operator ingesting POs, ASNs, invoices and remittance/deduction data, reconciling every deduction against PO, ASN and read-only 3PL/carrier data, classifying valid vs disputable and drafting dispute packets with evidence. Also acknowledges POs (accept/partial/backorder), builds ASNs from 3PL files and warns on at-risk ship windows.

**Killer example:** User: 'Why did Amazon pay us $41k less than invoiced last month, and what can we fight?' AI reconciles remittance to POs and ASNs: '$41.2k in deductions. $17.9k is ASN-mismatch on 23 POs; for 19 of them your 3PL's BOL shows full quantity delivered, so they are disputable. $9.4k is a valid late-ship charge on 6 POs.' It drafts 19 dispute packets with BOL, POD and ASN references and queues the 4 open POs whose ship window closes in 3 days.

**Integration path:** SP-API vendor groups plus Reports API for sales, inventory and payment/deduction data; OAuth/LWA with vendor roles. Dispute filing has no clean API, so the AI prepares packets and the user submits. Risks: vendor role approval and account-varying deduction reports.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 11. Mirakl Marketplace (seller side, across retailer-operated marketplaces such as Best Buy, Macy's, Lowe's, Carrefour, Leroy Merlin) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** One seller integration reaches many large retailers' customers off Amazon, via a REST API for products, offers, orders and shipments.

**Why people bounce off it:** Each operator defines its own attribute schema and value lists. Imports fail row by row with cryptic errors. A seller on 6 marketplaces keeps 6 divergent CSV templates and fixes rejections in spreadsheets; most abandon after operator two or three.

**AI operator:** Onboarding operator that pulls each operator's hierarchy, attributes and value lists, maps the master catalog (Shopify, Akeneo, CSV) to each schema, reads import error reports and proposes fixes, and remembers every fix as a reusable rule so each next operator onboards faster. Large writes are dry-run diffs with human approval.

**Killer example:** User: 'We just got approved on Lowe's. Get our 1,800 SKUs live; we sell the same on Best Buy.' AI pulls Lowe's category tree and attributes, auto-maps 1,540 SKUs reusing the Best Buy color and unit mappings, and flags 260 (90 need a Wattage value from a closed list). It proposes normalized values, shows the diff, submits after approval, polls the import, and re-submits the 31 failed rows with corrected values, then posts margin-adjusted offers.

**Integration path:** Mirakl seller REST API (P41/P42/P44 product import and error reports, OF01-OF03 offers, operator catalog endpoints, orders, shipments). Per-operator API key; no scraping. Python/TS service with LLM tool layer, schema-mapping store and dry-run diffs. Caveat: operator API versions differ and real seller accounts are needed to test.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 12. Microsoft 365 Apps policy and deployment layer (Office Deployment Tool config.xml, Office ADMX / Trust Center policies, macro blocking, update channels, add-in inventory) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Office policy governs the most-used software in every company, with thousands of ADMX policies deployable through GPO, Intune and Cloud Policy Service.

**Why people bounce off it:** Obscurely named per-app, per-version, per-user/machine policies with unclear conflict resolution, plus ODT configuration.xml quirks. Small IT admins copy half-understood checklists or leave insecure defaults like internet macros.

**AI operator:** Admin states intent in plain language; the operator maps it to ADMX policy names, registry paths and ODT XML, diffs against applied policy, explains each setting and what could break, and emits an Intune profile, GPO import or configuration.xml with pilot ring and rollback. Reads add-in and macro usage to warn before blocking anything.

**Killer example:** User: 'Stop macros from emailed attachments, but finance group GRP-Fin must still run their signed month-end workbook. Make everyone default to Monthly Enterprise Channel.' AI finds the Excel/Word/PowerPoint block-internet-macros policies, builds a Strict profile for all users plus an exception profile scoped to GRP-Fin trusting the finance cert thumbprint, emits configuration.xml with Channel=MonthlyEnterprise, dry-runs a diff showing 3 conflicting GPO values, assigns to a 20-device pilot ring and prints rollback steps.

**Integration path:** Intune Graph (groupPolicyConfigurations, configurationPolicies), generated ODT XML and ADMX-driven .reg/GPO files, thin Cloud Policy Service via Graph beta. Offline MVP built from published ADMX/ADML files needs no tenant. Intune in general is covered elsewhere; angle is the Office-specific translator and conflict explainer.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 3/5**
