### 1. Unity `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Full real-time production stack — Animator state machines and blend trees, Timeline/Cinemachine sequencing, URP/HDRP render pipelines, Addressables streaming, physics, and one-click builds to ~20 platforms. Millions of developers, the default engine for mobile and indie games, plus a growing AEC/automotive real-time-viz install base.

**Why people bounce off it:** The Animator Controller graph (layers, blend trees, transition exit-time and interruption-source), the Addressables Groups window with its Build Path / Load Path profile variables, and per-platform Player Settings with texture compression overrides (ASTC vs ETC2). A non-programmer can place a cube in a scene but cannot get a character to blend walk-to-run, and cannot get a build under the Play Store 200MB delivery limit.

**AI operator:** An in-Editor chat panel pre-loaded with Unity skills that reads the open scene and project graph and executes real Editor operations — wiring Animator states, creating and labeling Addressables groups, fixing import settings, authoring Timeline tracks, running batch builds — with every action expressed as an undoable Editor command plus a diff of the changed `.asset` / `.meta` YAML.

**Killer example:** User: "my character T-poses when she starts running, and the Android build is 400MB." AI inspects the Animator Controller, adds a 1D blend tree on `Speed` with correct thresholds, fixes the missing Has Exit Time transition, switches texture import to ASTC 6x6, splits the audio bank into a remote Addressables group, runs `-batchmode -executeMethod Build.Android`, and reports the new APK size.

**Integration path:** UnityEditor C# API (`EditorApplication`, `AssetDatabase`, `SerializedObject`, `AnimatorController` / `AddressableAssetSettings`) driven from a custom `EditorWindow` bridge; headless `Unity -batchmode -quit -executeMethod Class.Method`; the official Unity CLI (`com.unity.pipeline`, Unity 6.0 LTS+) that registers terminal-invokable commands against a live Editor; Package Manager plus direct YAML asset/`.meta` edits.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 2. Walmart Marketplace (Seller Center) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Full seller stack — category-specific rich item setup, Walmart Fulfillment Services, Repricer, promotions and rollbacks, offer (Buy Box) management, Inventory Recommendations with demand forecasts, and near-real-time `ORDER_UPDATES` webhooks. The #2 US marketplace and the one most Amazon sellers are being pushed to expand into.

**Why people bounce off it:** Item Spec v4.x bulk feeds. The seller must pick the exact `productType`, satisfy that type's required *and* conditional attribute set (nested JSON like `MPItem.Visible.<category>`), then decode ingestion failures in the Activity Feed dashboard where errors come back as bare attribute-path codes. Price, inventory, promo and item content each live in a *different* feed type (`MP_ITEM`, `MP_WFS_ITEM`, `MP_MAINTENANCE`), so one logical change means three separate submissions.

**AI operator:** A chat operator pre-loaded with the Item Spec 4.x schema, feed-type routing and Walmart's error-code catalog. The merchant pastes a supplier CSV or points at their Shopify catalog and talks; the AI picks the `productType`, maps attributes, submits feeds, polls status, and auto-repairs and resubmits the rejected rows instead of the merchant hand-editing spreadsheets for a week.

**Killer example:** User: "list these 40 SKUs as seller-fulfilled with 2-day shipping, match my Shopify titles, and fix whatever Walmart rejects." AI classifies each SKU to a `productType`, builds an `MP_ITEM` feed, POSTs Bulk Item Setup, polls the feed status endpoint, rewrites the attributes that threw errors, and resubmits until all items are published.

**Integration path:** Walmart Marketplace REST APIs (developer.walmart.com): Bulk Item Setup `POST /v3/feeds?feedType=MP_ITEM|MP_WFS_ITEM|MP_MAINTENANCE`, `GET /v3/feeds/{feedId}` for status, Price / Inventory / Promotions APIs, Orders API, `ORDER_UPDATES` webhook, OAuth2 token endpoint with Client ID/Secret.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 3. Final Cut Pro `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Magnetic Timeline with connected clips and compound clips, Roles/subroles-driven audio mixing and multi-version export, multicam angle sync, Smart Collections over keyword and metadata, Object Tracker, and Compressor/Motion template integration. Enormous prosumer, YouTube and corporate-video install base on Mac.

**Why people bounce off it:** Roles and subroles — users have no idea that Dialogue/Music/Effects roles are what drive audio lanes, stems, and the Export "Roles as multitrack" option, so they hand-balance every clip. Second wall: the Libraries/Events/managed-vs-external media model that strands people on "Missing File" proxies. Third: connected-clip trimming that shifts everything downstream when they ripple.

**AI operator:** A Workflow Extension living inside the FCP window that takes plain-language editorial direction, reads the current project as FCPXML, and writes back a restructured timeline — rough cuts from transcript, role assignment and stems, ducking keyframes, caption tracks, export presets — showing a before/after timeline preview before committing.

**Killer example:** User: "cut this 40-minute interview into a 3-minute highlight reel, put the music under at -18dB with ducking, and give me a vertical version." AI parses transcript ranges, generates FCPXML 1.13 with keyword ranges, a spine of selected clips, Dialogue/Music role assignments, audio keyframe ducking and a 9:16 Transform/Smart Conform variant, then drops both projects into the open Library.

**Integration path:** FCPXML 1.9–1.13 import/export (Apple ProApps FCPXML reference) as the read/write surface; Workflow Extensions SDK (`ProExtensionHost` framework, `NSPasteboardItemDataProvider` drag-to-timeline); the `.fcpbundle` library structure on disk; Motion `.motn` template parameters for generators/titles; AppleScript/Automator and the Compressor CLI (`Compressor -batchname`) for import and render.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 4. Palo Alto Networks Panorama / PAN-OS `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Panorama centrally manages fleets of PAN-OS firewalls with App-ID / User-ID / Content-ID security policy, decryption, NAT, security profile groups, GlobalProtect and SD-WAN, pushing config to hundreds of devices via device groups and template stacks. The dominant enterprise firewall platform.

**Why people bounce off it:** The two-axis hierarchy nobody holds in their head. Policy lives in device groups with a pre-rulebase / local-rules / post-rulebase sandwich and shared-vs-device-group object scoping, while interfaces, zones and routing live in *templates* merged through template stacks by priority. Add the candidate-config commit model (Commit to Panorama, then Push to Devices, with partial commits and admin locks) and "open port 8443 for the new app" becomes a scavenger hunt for which layer owns the object — plus a shadowed-rule surprise.

**AI operator:** A firewall change-request operator: the user describes the intended access in business terms, the AI resolves the correct device group and template stack, checks for shadowing and overlapping existing rules, creates address and service objects at the right scope, attaches the standard security profile group, writes the rule in the correct rulebase position, and returns a diff plus a scoped commit-and-push preview for human approval.

**Killer example:** User: "let the new Epic app servers 10.20.5.0/24 reach the Oracle cluster on 1521 from the DC-East firewalls, log at session end, standard threat profiles." AI creates the address group in the DC-East device group, adds a post-rulebase security rule with the `oracle` App-ID and the Outbound-Standard profile group, flags that existing rule `Any-DB-Allow` already shadows it, and stages a commit-all push scoped to just DC-East.

**Integration path:** PAN-OS XML API (`type=config` with XPath set/edit/get, `type=commit`, `commit-all` for Panorama pushes), PAN-OS REST API (`/restapi/v11.1/Objects/*`, `/Policies/SecurityPreRules` with device-group and vsys params), `pan-os-python` (pandevice) SDK, the `paloaltonetworks.panos` Ansible collection, and `PaloAltoNetworks/terraform-provider-panos` for plan/apply diffs.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 5. SAP S/4HANA `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Runs order-to-cash, procure-to-pay, production planning and financial close for most of the Fortune 500 on one integrated ledger. The Universal Journal (ACDOCA) unifies GL, AP/AR, asset accounting, controlling and margin analysis in one line-item table with real-time multi-ledger, multi-currency, parallel-valuation posting. Nothing else touches its depth of cross-module posting logic.

**Why people bounce off it:** You cannot do anything without knowing the transaction code and its field grammar — FB60 for a vendor invoice, F-28 for incoming payment clearing, MIRO for invoice verification, VA01 for a sales order, ME21N for a PO, MIGO for goods receipt (movement type 101 vs 103 vs 105). Each screen demands company code, posting key, document type, tax code and cost-object derivation (cost center vs profit center vs WBS vs order), and document splitting silently rejects postings whose characteristics don't balance. Fiori reskins the same field-level semantics without removing them, so AP clerks still get trained for weeks on "which T-code and which posting key."

**AI operator:** An S/4HANA operator preloaded with skills for the standard business documents and the Universal Journal data model: it translates plain-language business intent into validated API payloads (correct company code, document type, posting keys, tax code, cost object), simulates the posting, shows the resulting debit/credit lines for confirmation, then posts and returns the document number. It also answers "where did this number come from" by drilling ACDOCA instead of making the user learn the FAGLL03 selection screen.

**Killer example:** User: "Post the $48,000 Acme retainer as a vendor invoice against the Marketing cost center, accrue it evenly across Q3, and put it in the US ledger." AI builds an `API_SUPPLIERINVOICE_PROCESS_SRV` payload plus three accrual journal entries via `API_JOURNALENTRY_SRV`, checks document splitting will balance, posts, and returns the four SAP document numbers with a drill link.

**Integration path:** OData V2/V4 A2X APIs on SAP Business Accelerator Hub — `API_SUPPLIERINVOICE_PROCESS_SRV`, `API_JOURNALENTRY_SRV`, `API_SALES_ORDER_SRV`, `API_PURCHASEORDER_PROCESS_SRV`, `API_MATERIAL_DOCUMENT_SRV`, G/L Account Line Items (A2X) for reads — with a free self-serve API sandbox; plus classic BAPI/RFC (`BAPI_ACC_DOCUMENT_POST`, `BAPI_INCOMINGINVOICE_CREATE`) over the NetWeaver RFC SDK / PyRFC for on-prem, CDS view consumption, and SAP BTP / Cloud SDK for deployment inside the customer landscape.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 6. Trello `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Beyond cards and lists, Trello carries Butler automation (rules, card and board buttons, due-date and calendar commands), Custom Fields, Advanced Checklists with per-item assignees and dates, card templates, and Power-Ups — enough to run a real intake-to-delivery workflow. Tens of millions of users and the default board tool at countless SMBs, school and office teams.

**Why people bounce off it:** The Butler automation builder. A rule must be assembled out of Butler's own trigger/action grammar ("when a card is moved into list QA by anyone", "set due date to the next Monday", `{cardname}` / `{listbefore}` variables), the user must pick between the Rules / Card Button / Board Button / Due Date / Calendar tabs, and wire it to Custom Fields. Result: 95% of boards are hand-dragged, and the automation, custom fields and Advanced Checklists that make Trello powerful are never touched.

**AI operator:** A Trello operator that designs and installs whole workflows from a description — creating lists, labels and Custom Fields, converting spoken rules into executed automation (Butler-style rules where writable, otherwise an equivalent webhook-driven rule engine the agent runs), building card templates and Advanced Checklists — plus bulk board surgery (split a 400-card board by label into two boards, archive stale cards, re-point due dates) and plain-language board reporting.

**Killer example:** User: "When a card sits in QA more than 3 days, move it to Blocked, add the QA lead, set the Blocked Since custom field, and comment @team." AI creates the Blocked list and a date Custom Field via `/1/customFields`, registers a webhook on the board, runs the rule against every card nightly, and writes out the equivalent Butler rule text for the user to paste if they want it native.

**Integration path:** Trello REST API v1 (`api.trello.com/1/boards`, `/cards`, `/lists`, `/checklists`, `/customFields`, `/actions`), board webhooks, and the Power-Up capability SDK (`client.js`, card-badges / card-detail-badges) with an API key issued through `trello.com/apps/admin`.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 7. Typeform `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Logic Jumps, calculator Variables (score/price), Recall Info for personalized question text, Hidden Fields and URL parameters, outcome-based quiz endings, payment fields and per-form webhooks — enough to build routing quizzes, lead qualifiers and self-serve pricing calculators. Very widely used by marketing, HR and ops teams.

**Why people bounce off it:** The Logic map editor. Once a form has more than three branches, the condition rows ("If Q2 is equal to Enterprise then jump to Q9"), the Variables tab with add/subtract/multiply operations, Recall Info references and Hidden Field wiring become a tangle that ordinary marketers abandon — they publish a linear form and lose the qualification logic entirely.

**AI operator:** An AI form operator that compiles a described qualification flow directly into Typeform's logic JSON — branches, score/price variables, recall, hidden UTM fields, outcome endings — validates that every path terminates, then edits it conversationally ("add an SMB path that skips pricing") and reads results back ("which branch drops off most?") without the user ever opening the Logic map.

**Killer example:** User: "Make a lead-qual form that skips pricing for students, scores budget + timeline into a fit score, sends anything over 8 to the demo-booking ending, and carries `utm_source` through." AI POSTs a form to the Create API with fields, logic jumps, a `fit_score` variable, `hidden: [utm_source]` and multiple `thankyou_screens`, returning a live URL.

**Integration path:** Typeform Create API (`POST`/`PUT https://api.typeform.com/forms` with `fields`, `logic`, `variables`, `hidden` arrays), Responses API `GET /forms/{id}/responses`, Webhooks API, OAuth2 apps.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 8. BambooHR `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Employee records with unlimited custom fields and custom tables, time-off policies with accrual periods and milestones, onboarding/offboarding task lists, e-signature, performance reviews, and a report builder that can slice every field in the system. Tens of thousands of small and mid-sized companies run their entire HR record-of-truth on it.

**Why people bounce off it:** Two screens break ordinary HR admins. The Access Levels matrix — per-field View/Edit permissions scoped to employee populations, which silently hides fields from shared reports and can block employees from seeing their own time-off balance. And the time-off accrual policy editor — accrual periods, milestones, carryover caps, proration, and a locked Accrual Schedule you must explicitly unlock to edit. Admins routinely ship policies that accrue the wrong balance with no way to see why.

**AI operator:** An HR-admin-in-chat that configures BambooHR from a description: it builds time-off policies and *simulates the resulting balances before saving*, constructs custom access levels field-by-field, turns a plain-English question into a custom report, and runs bulk employee field updates and onboarding assignments with a preview diff.

**Killer example:** User: "Give German employees 28 days accruing monthly from their start date, 5 days carryover that expires March 31, and let managers see compensation for their direct reports only." AI creates the accrual policy with the right accrual periods and carryover rules, assigns it to the German population via the time-off policy endpoints, and builds a custom Access Level with Compensation set to View scoped to direct reports.

**Integration path:** BambooHR REST API v1 at `api.bamboohr.com` with API-key Basic auth — `/v1/employees` and `/v1/employees/{id}` (GET/POST/PUT across arbitrary fields and custom tables), `/v1/reports/custom` for ad-hoc reports in JSON/CSV/XLS, the time-off request/history/"assign time off policies for an employee" v1.1 endpoints, employee files and ATS endpoints — plus registered webhooks for new hires, terminations and field changes.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 9. AWS Glue `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Serverless Spark ETL with a visual Studio canvas, crawlers that auto-infer schemas into the Glue Data Catalog (the metastore Athena, Redshift Spectrum and EMR all read), job bookmarks for incremental loads, DynamicFrames that survive schema drift, built-in scheduling and triggers, and Data Quality rules.

**Why people bounce off it:** The non-data part kills ordinary analysts — the IAM role + VPC + Glue Connection + self-referencing security-group rule triangle required before a JDBC source will even connect. Then crawler classifiers silently create duplicate or wrongly-partitioned tables, job bookmark semantics re-process or skip data, worker type/DPU sizing is a guess, and `resolveChoice` on DynamicFrames is mandatory whenever the crawler guessed a column as both string and long.

**AI operator:** A natural-language data-pipeline operator for Glue: the user states source, destination and business rule, and the AI provisions the crawler and Catalog tables, generates a real Glue Studio *visual* job (registered as a DAG so the user sees and can edit the canvas, not an opaque script), picks the IAM role and connection, sets bookmarks, partitions and worker sizing, runs it, then reads CloudWatch job-run errors and repairs the job itself.

**Killer example:** User: "every morning take the new order files landing in `s3://raw/orders`, join them to the customers table in Redshift, drop test accounts, and write partitioned Parquet to the curated bucket." AI calls `CreateCrawler` on the raw prefix, then `CreateJob` with `codeGenConfigurationNodes` (S3 source → Join → Filter → ChangeSchema → S3 target with `partitionKeys=[order_date]`), enables job bookmarks, sets G.1X workers, and wires a scheduled Trigger.

**Integration path:** AWS Glue API via boto3/AWS CLI — `CreateCrawler`, `CreateJob`/`UpdateJob` with the `CodeGenConfigurationNodes` DAG structure (which registers the job in Glue Studio's visual editor), `StartJobRun`, `GetJobRun`, Data Catalog APIs (`CreateTable`/`GetTables`), Glue Data Quality rulesets, plus CloudWatch Logs for error feedback.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 10. Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** One account covers bulk email campaigns, transactional email, SMS/WhatsApp, a visual marketing-automation workflow builder, e-commerce event tracking, landing pages and a light CRM — used by hundreds of thousands of SMBs as their whole marketing stack.

**Why people bounce off it:** The automation workflow editor — entry points vs conditions vs wait steps — compounded by the fact that behavioral triggers only fire if the tracker script and custom events are installed correctly, so workflows silently never run. On top of that the segment builder forces users to understand attribute types (TEXT / CATEGORY / DATE / TRANSACTIONAL attributes from an order sync), plus double-opt-in list plumbing and sender DKIM/SPF authentication before anything sends at all.

**AI operator:** "Describe the campaign, get the automation" — an operator that builds segments from order and event data, generates the email and SMS templates, assembles the multi-step workflow, checks sender authentication and list consent state before sending, and explains in plain language why a given contact did or didn't receive a message.

**Killer example:** User: "anyone who bought coffee beans 25+ days ago and hasn't reordered gets a 3-step refill series, SMS on step 3 with a 10% code." AI builds the segment from purchase events, creates the templates, wires the wait/condition workflow, and schedules it inside quiet-hour limits.

**Integration path:** Brevo REST API v3 (`https://api.brevo.com/v3`): `/contacts`, `/contacts/lists`, `/contacts/segments`, `/contacts/attributes`, `/emailCampaigns`, `/smtp/email` and `/smtp/templates`, `/transactionalSMS/send`, `/events` (custom events for automation triggers), `/senders` and domain-authentication endpoints, plus webhooks and official SDKs in 7 languages.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 11. Oracle Primavera P6 EPPM `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** A true CPM engine and the contractual scheduling standard for megaprojects — 50k+ activity networks, relationship types with lags, multiple activity and resource calendars, resource/role loading and leveling, multi-baseline variance and earned value, activity codes and UDFs, risk registers, and an enterprise EPS/OBS hierarchy spanning a whole portfolio. The XER file is a deliverable owners and claims consultants contractually demand.

**Why people bounce off it:** The scheduling internals leak straight into the UI. The F9 Schedule → Options dialog forces a choice of retained logic vs progress override; constraints like "Finish On or After" silently override logic; total float vs free float diverge after multi-calendar lag interpretation; there are four different % complete types (Duration/Units/Physical/Scope); and seeing which activities actually drove a date change requires building a custom Layout plus Filter. Field engineers open the Activities view with 100+ columns and give up, so every project keeps a dedicated P6 scheduler on payroll.

**AI operator:** A "talk to your schedule" operator holding P6 scheduling semantics as skills: it answers driving-path and delay-impact questions, posts progress (actual start/finish, remaining duration), reruns the scheduler, resource-levels, spins up what-if copies with resequenced logic, and writes the monthly baseline-variance narrative the PM currently hand-types.

**Killer example:** PM says: "The concrete pour slipped 5 days — what happens to substantial completion, and give me a recovery option that doesn't add weekend crews." AI posts actual dates to the activity, reruns Schedule with retained logic, traces the longest path, and returns a what-if project copy converting two FS relationships to SS with lag, plus a baseline variance summary: "finish moves 6 days; the driving path shifted from MEP rough-in to the rebar chain."

**Integration path:** P6 EPPM REST API (`/p6ws/restapi/` — `/project`, `/activity`, `/relationship`, `/resourceAssignment`, `/baseline`, `/eps`, scheduling and checkIn/checkOut endpoints, documented through release 26), the legacy P6 EPPM SOAP Web Services (ProjectService/ActivityService/ScheduleService) for gaps, the Primavera Cloud REST API for the SaaS line, the Java Integration API, and XER / P6-XML import-export for bulk round-trips.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 12. Dropbox Business `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Versioned sync with 180-day rewind, team folders in a shared team space, granular per-member folder ACLs, link-expiry and password policies, file requests, legal holds, and admin-wide retention and sharing policy controls. Almost every SMB and a large share of enterprises already pay for it.

**Why people bounce off it:** The team space vs member-folder namespace model. Ordinary users cannot tell a "team folder" from a "shared folder", the Manage Access dialog mixes inherited and explicit members with no explanation, and the switches that actually matter — who can add members, who can create shared links, link expiry defaults — are scattered across Admin console → Settings → Sharing, per-folder Settings, and per-link settings. So admins default to "share with everyone" and leave it there.

**AI operator:** A Dropbox-connected agent with preloaded skills for the namespace/ACL model that turns plain-language access requests into correct team-folder + member + policy operations, runs recurring access reviews ("who outside the company can see Finance?"), executes time-boxed grants with automatic revocation, and does bulk restructuring — move a project tree into the team space, re-permission it, fix broken shared links — as one reviewable plan with a dry-run diff before it touches anything.

**Killer example:** User: "Give the Acme audit firm view-only access to 2026 Financials for 30 days and make sure nobody outside the company can generate links to it." AI calls `/2/team/team_folder/create-or-locate`, adds the auditors via `/2/sharing/add_folder_member` at `access_level=viewer`, sets `/2/sharing/update_folder_policy` to `shared_link_policy=team` + `acl_update_policy=owner`, and schedules `/2/sharing/remove_folder_member` in 30 days.

**Integration path:** Dropbox API v2 HTTP endpoints — `/2/files/*`, `/2/sharing/add_folder_member`, `/2/sharing/update_folder_policy`, `/2/sharing/list_shared_links`, `/2/team/team_folder/create`, `/2/team/members/list`, `/2/team/namespaces/list` — with admin operations via the `Dropbox-API-Select-Admin` header, scoped OAuth2 apps and webhooks.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**
