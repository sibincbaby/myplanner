### 1. Atlassian Cloud organization admin (admin.atlassian.com): managed accounts, product access, groups, seat reclamation, API-token and audit-log hygiene `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** The org-level control plane covers every Atlassian product at once. Its Admin APIs expose managed users with per-product last-active timestamps, group membership, product access, SCIM provisioning and the org audit log.

**Why people bounce off it:** Seat costs scale per user across Jira, Confluence, JSM and Compass, and prices rise annually. The admin UI shows one product and one page at a time, so 'who is paying for a seat they don't use?' means exporting CSVs per product and cross-referencing in a spreadsheet. Group-to-product-access rules and suspended vs deactivated vs deleted semantics are easy to get wrong, and a mistake silently removes someone's access.

**AI operator:** A 'Seat & Access Operator' that builds a unified per-user, per-product activity table, proposes a reclaim plan with dollar savings from list pricing, and stages it as a reviewable batch with a rollback file. It also runs the reverse flow (onboard a contractor with the right groups) and verifies by re-reading org state.

**Killer example:** User: 'Find everyone who hasn't touched Jira or Confluence in 90 days, tell me what we'd save at list price, and pull their product access but keep the accounts for 30 days.' AI: 'Found 143 inactive paid seats across 2 products, about 24k USD/yr at list price. Biggest pockets: Marketing (31), Contractors (27), ex-Acquisition-Co (22). 9 of them are Jira admins or sole project leads, so I excluded them and flagged them for you. Here is the remaining 134 grouped by department. On your OK I will remove them from the product-access groups, tag the batch reclaim-2026-11, write rollback.csv, and draft the heads-up email to their managers. Nothing is deleted.' After confirming, it executes, re-reads the org to verify the seat count dropped by 134, and schedules a 30-day reminder to deactivate.

**Integration path:** Atlassian Admin REST API (org-scoped API key) plus Jira/Confluence user-search APIs; SCIM if an IdP is configured. Reads are well covered; some policy surfaces are GET-only, so the MVP is seat and access reclaim plus audit-log search. Atlassian also ships a remote MCP server, so this is a skill pack on top of it.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 2. Shiprocket (with Delhivery/Shipway-style Indian shipping aggregators): NDR, RTO and COD operations `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** One panel and API multiplexes 25+ couriers with pincode serviceability, AWB assignment, pickup scheduling, COD remittance, wallet billing, NDR actions and return orders. For COD-heavy D2C brands it directly controls margin, since RTO rates of 20-30% eat profit.

**Why people bounce off it:** Hundreds of NDR tickets sit in a table and each needs a per-row reattempt, reschedule or call action within 1-2 attempts or the parcel is RTO'd. Courier choice is a pincode x weight x COD x courier-score tradeoff, and built-in priority rules are coarse. COD remittance is reconciled by hand and weight disputes are a separate flow. Most sellers leave couriers on 'recommended' and bleed RTO and freight money.

**AI operator:** A logistics copilot that (1) triages NDRs by reason and takes the right action per group, handing off to WhatsApp/call outreach, (2) learns delivery and RTO rates per pincode cluster from the seller's history and picks the courier per order, (3) reconciles COD remittance against delivered AWBs, (4) packages weight disputes. Every write is a dry-run diff with wallet spend caps.

**Killer example:** User: 'Everything stuck in NDR with 2+ attempts in Maharashtra and Karnataka: reattempt where the customer said they're available, and for the rest cancel and re-ship via whichever courier has the best past delivery rate to that pincode.' AI: 'Pulled 62 open NDRs. 41 customers replied yes, so I'll reattempt those tomorrow 10-1. 12 have incomplete addresses, so I drafted a WhatsApp asking for a corrected address and held them. 9 are refusals or no response: cancelling and re-creating via Xpressbees (87% delivery to those pincodes vs 61% for Delhivery), freight +Rs 11 per order. Projected RTO avoided: about Rs 14,800 this week. Approve?' On approval it calls the NDR-action and order-create endpoints and logs every AWB changed.

**Integration path:** Shiprocket public REST API (apiv2.shiprocket.in/v1/external) with token auth: orders, serviceability, AWB, pickup, tracking, NDR list and action, returns, wallet. Webhooks for tracking. COD remittance may need report export. MCP server plus small web chat in 2-3 weeks.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 3. Open Policy Agent (OPA) / Rego, with Conftest, Gatekeeper and Kyverno as enforcement points (policy-as-code for Kubernetes, Terraform plans, CI configs and APIs) `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** OPA is a CNCF graduated engine behind Gatekeeper, Conftest and Styra. One engine enforces 'no public S3 buckets', 'pods have resource limits', 'approved base images only' and 'no wildcard IAM' across admission, Terraform plans and CI. Platform and security teams need this and rarely have anyone who can author it.

**Why people bounce off it:** Rego is a Datalog-derived language with unusual semantics (partial rules, implicit iteration, undefined vs false). A correct policy also needs positive and negative tests, realistic input JSON, and Gatekeeper boilerplate. A typo in an input path makes a policy silently match nothing, which looks identical to a passing policy. Most engineers write two policies and avoid it.

**AI operator:** A 'Policy Pilot' preloaded with Rego idioms and input schemas for K8s admission, Terraform plan JSON and CI configs. From plain English it drafts Rego, generates positive/negative fixtures from real inputs, runs opa test and opa eval --explain, and proves the policy actually fires on the negative fixture (killing vacuous passes). It back-tests against existing manifests/plans for blast radius and emits Gatekeeper/Kyverno/Conftest wrappers in warn-only mode.

**Killer example:** User: 'Block any Terraform change that opens port 22 or 3389 to 0.0.0.0/0, but allow it in the sandbox account.' AI writes Rego against the terraform show -json schema covering inline aws_security_group rules, aws_security_group_rule and IPv6 ::/0, builds 6 fixtures (2 violations, 2 passes, sandbox exception, IPv6), and runs opa test: 6/6 pass, and it shows the policy fires on both violation fixtures so it is not vacuous. It then runs the rule against the last 30 plan files in the repo: '3 merged PRs would have been blocked' with the exact resources listed. Finally it opens a PR adding the policy in warn mode with a conftest CI step and a flip-to-deny checklist.

**Integration path:** opa and conftest CLIs are fully headless (opa eval/test/check/fmt, OPA server REST /v1/policies, /v1/data). Gatekeeper CRDs via kubectl, terraform show -json. Mostly a codegen-plus-test-loop harness over files and CLIs; ships as a Claude Code skill, MCP server wrapping opa, or GitHub App.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 4. Amazon EC2 Auto Scaling + Launch Templates + Elastic Load Balancing (ASG fleets behind ALB/NLB target groups) `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** EC2 plus Auto Scaling is still the compute substrate under a large share of AWS production: monoliths, batch fleets, Spot services and Kubernetes node groups. The surface is large: launch template versions, mixed instances, Spot strategies, target-tracking and step scaling, scheduled actions, warm pools, lifecycle hooks, instance refresh.

**Why people bounce off it:** Behaviour is spread across five or more console screens that interact non-obviously. Instance Refresh settings (MinHealthyPercentage, warmup, checkpoints) decide whether a rollout is safe. Default vs $Latest template pointers cause silent drift, target tracking fights scheduled actions, grace periods kill slow-booting instances in a loop, and launch failures show up as terse activity-history lines.

**AI operator:** An 'ASG Pilot' that reads the whole fleet topology (ASG, template versions, target groups, policies, schedules, alarms, hooks), answers causal questions, and executes changes as reviewed plans: AMI rollouts with canary checkpoints, on-demand to Spot-mix conversion, right-sizing, and diagnosing health-check loops. Emits an API diff or Terraform/CloudFormation patch for IaC-managed groups and requires approval for writes.

**Killer example:** User: 'Roll the web fleet to the new golden AMI with no more than 10% capacity out at once, and abort automatically if ALB 5xx goes up.' AI: 'web-asg runs 24 instances on lt-web v17 ($Default=v15, so there is drift I will fix). I created v18 with ami-0b3f from build tag 2026-11-12, set MinHealthyPercentage 90, and a 300s warmup measured from your last 20 boot times (p95 = 262s). Checkpoints at 20/50/100%, plus a CloudWatch alarm on HTTPCode_Target_5XX_Count > 1% for 3 min wired as the auto-rollback. Your ASG is managed by Terraform, so here is the plan diff to commit afterwards. Start?' It then pauses at the first checkpoint reporting p99 flat and 0 unhealthy targets, and cancels and reverts the default version if the alarm fires.

**Integration path:** Full AWS API coverage (StartInstanceRefresh, UpdateAutoScalingGroup, PutScalingPolicy, CreateLaunchTemplateVersion, DescribeTargetHealth, CloudWatch) via boto3/CLI/MCP with a read-only role and a separate gated write role, plus a Terraform/CloudFormation patch path to avoid drift.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 5. Office File Doctor: Excel/Word/PowerPoint (OOXML .xlsx/.docx/.pptx) bloat, slowness and corruption repair `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** Office files hold a decade of business logic. OOXML is an open zip of XML parts (ECMA-376), so every cause of bloat is inspectable: styles, conditional-format rules, used ranges, pivot caches, calc chain, media, layouts.

**Why people bounce off it:** Office gives no 'why is this file slow, huge or broken?' view. Typical culprits are 40k duplicated styles, thousands of copy-pasted CF rules, a used range out to row 1,048,576, volatile OFFSET/INDIRECT chains, duplicate pivot caches or hidden 4K images. 'We found a problem with some content' gives no cause, so users rebuild by hand or email the file around in fear.

**AI operator:** A headless operator unzips the file, runs deterministic analyzers (part sizes, style count, CF dedupe, used-range vs real data, volatile graph, pivot-cache sharing, image DPI, orphan layouts, Open XML SDK validation), and builds a ranked plain-English 'why' report with a proposed diff of safe fixes. It applies them via XML surgery/openpyxl/python-pptx and proves equivalence by recalculating before and after in LibreOffice and diffing every cell.

**Killer example:** User: 'Q3-forecast.xlsx is 74 MB, takes 3 minutes to open, and Excel says it found unreadable content. Fix it, don't change any results.' AI: 'Found 61,204 duplicate cell styles, 18,900 fragmented CF rules (collapsible to 14), used range inflated to row 1,048,576 on 3 sheets, 11 full-column OFFSETs recalculating on every edit, and a corrupt drawing part (dangling rId7). I deduped styles, merged the CF rules, trimmed ranges, swapped OFFSET for INDEX, and removed the dangling relationship. The file is now 6.1 MB and opens in 4s. I recalculated all 212,000 formula cells before and after: 0 values differ. Original kept as Q3-forecast.orig.xlsx.'

**Integration path:** No app API needed; the OOXML zip is the interface, so it runs headless on Linux or as a SaaS upload flow. Python (zipfile, lxml, openpyxl, python-pptx), Open XML SDK validator, LibreOffice headless for recalc checks. MVP is a CLI/web drop zone for .xlsx; optional MCP server with analyze_file, propose_fixes, apply_with_verification.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 6. Apache Kafka cluster operations (brokers, topics, consumer groups, ACLs, quotas; Confluent / MSK / Redpanda / Strimzi, with AKHQ or Kafka UI) `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** Kafka is the event backbone at most mid-size and large engineering orgs, and almost every production incident touches it: lag, rebalances, under-replicated partitions, retention, compaction.

**Why people bounce off it:** Tooling is a pile of kafka-*.sh scripts with long flag lists and hand-written JSON reassignment plans. Offset resets are destructive and easy to get wrong (--to-datetime vs --shift-by, dry-run vs execute). ACLs are written one tuple at a time, and UIs show state but won't plan or explain a change. Teams have one 'Kafka person'; everyone else opens a ticket.

**AI operator:** A 'Kafka Pilot' on the Admin API that does lag and rebalance triage, topic provisioning to a naming/retention policy, least-privilege ACL generation, safe offset resets (always dry-run with a before/after diff), and throttled partition reassignment verified afterward. Destructive operations need an explicit approval token.

**Killer example:** User: 'The payments-reconciler group is 4M messages behind on orders.v2 since 2am; skip the poison message but don't lose anything else.' AI finds one of 12 partitions stuck at offset 8,113,402 on a deserialization failure, shows the record's key, timestamp and size, proposes resetting only that partition to offset+1 (dry-run diff: 11 other partitions untouched), and after confirmation applies it with the group stopped, watches lag drain, and writes an incident note with the skipped offset so the record can be replayed to a DLQ.

**Integration path:** Kafka AdminClient (Java, confluent-kafka-python, kafkajs) covers topics, configs, ACLs, groups, offsets, reassignments, quotas; Confluent REST v3, MSK via same API plus AWS API, Schema Registry REST. MCP/CLI with plan/apply tools; local-first packaging since it needs cluster credentials.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 7. Apache Fineract (Mifos X / Mifos Web App): open-source core banking and microfinance platform `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** A full multi-tenant core banking system: loans, savings, shares, groups and centers, double-entry GL and reporting. Deployed by many MFIs, credit unions and fintechs across Africa, South Asia and Latin America, free to self-host.

**Why people bounce off it:** Enormous configuration surface: loan products (amortization, interest calc, repayment strategy), charges, accounting rules (cash vs accrual, per-product GL mapping), calendars, batch jobs, spread across dozens of screens. Misconfigured GL mappings corrupt the ledger silently, so small MFIs pay consultants for setup and dread changes.

**AI operator:** A product and accounting configuration copilot that translates a plain-language lending policy into loan product JSON, charges and GL mappings, simulates the repayment schedule and journal entries before saving, and handles bulk group onboarding, loan rescheduling, batch jobs and portfolio-at-risk questions.

**Killer example:** User: 'Create a 12-week group loan for women's savings groups: 24% flat annual interest, weekly repayments, 1% processing fee at disbursement, 3 grace days before penalties, accrual accounting. Then onboard the 14 groups in this CSV.' AI creates the charge and product, maps accrual GL accounts (interest receivable, fee income, portfolio, penalty income), posts a sample 10,000 loan to show the 12 weekly instalments and every journal entry for sign-off, then creates 14 groups, members and center assignments and returns the rows it rejected with reasons.

**Integration path:** API-first with Swagger/OpenAPI, basic/OAuth2 with tenant header, command endpoints with validation. Docker Compose demo with seed data; MVP is an MCP server wrapping about 40 endpoints with plan-then-apply showing the JSON before POST.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 8. Arelle (open-source XBRL/iXBRL platform), with Workiva Wdesk as a second connector `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** The reference open-source XBRL engine behind much digital financial reporting. iXBRL tagging and validation are mandatory for EU ESEF issuers, SEC 10-K/10-Q filers and CSRD/ESRS statements. It validates calculation linkbases, dimensions and filing rules, with a plugin system.

**Why people bounce off it:** Tagging line items to the right taxonomy concept with correct dimensions, sign, decimals and scale is tedious, and validation errors (calc inconsistency, missing dimension, wrong period type, duplicate fact) are cryptic. Rolling forward each quarter is mostly manual.

**AI operator:** A tagging and validation copilot that reads the report, proposes concepts per line item (US-GAAP, IFRS, ESRS), injects ix:nonFraction markup, runs Arelle, reads the error codes and fixes the markup. It keeps per-company tagging memory so roll-forwards re-tag automatically, with source text and confidence shown for sign-off.

**Killer example:** User: 'Roll our Q2 10-Q iXBRL forward to Q3 using the numbers in this Excel, flag new line items, and give me a validation report for the auditor.' AI loads the prior filing's fact map, swaps in Q3 values with correct contexts and durations, tags the new 'Restructuring reserve' row with a proposed us-gaap concept and a custom-extension fallback, runs Arelle with the EDGAR plugin, fixes 3 rounding-scale calc errors, and outputs a tag diff versus Q2 plus a clean validation log.

**Integration path:** arelleCmdLine and Python API for headless runs; JSON/CSV facts, validation logs, plugin hooks. Workiva REST API and Wdata for the second connector. MVP: MCP server over Arelle with a taxonomy-concept search index and a human review UI per tag.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 9. Salesforce CRM Analytics (formerly Tableau CRM / Einstein Analytics): datasets, recipes, SAQL, dashboard JSON `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** The analytics engine inside a very large Salesforce install base. Joins CRM data with external datasets, runs recipes, SAQL and interactive dashboards with bindings, and answers cross-object cohort, pipeline velocity and multi-hop win-rate questions that standard Reports cannot.

**Why people bounce off it:** Dashboards are hand-wired JSON (widgets, steps, cell bindings, faceting groups) plus SAQL, a pipeline language nothing else teaches. Recipes fail silently when schemas drift. Admins who can build standard reports hit a wall and clone template dashboards or hire consultants.

**AI operator:** A dashboard and recipe operator that reads dataset schema (XMD), dashboard JSON and recipe nodes, writes SAQL steps, widgets and bindings, edits recipes, previews each step against live data with a diff before saving, and repairs dashboards after a field rename.

**Killer example:** A sales ops manager says: 'On the Pipeline dashboard, add a stacked bar of open amount by stage split by whether the deal had activity in the last 14 days, filtered by the region toggle, and grey out stages with fewer than 5 deals.' The AI writes a cogroup SAQL step, adds the widget and selection binding and conditional formatting, shows the JSON diff and a sample result, then saves a new dashboard version after approval.

**Integration path:** Analytics REST API (/wave/dashboards, /recipes, /datasets, /query) plus Metadata API types WaveDashboard, WaveRecipe, WaveXmd via sf CLI. MVP: Connected App OAuth, schema-validated dashboard patcher, SAQL dry-run via /wave/query; needs a corpus of exported dashboards since the JSON schema is partly documented.

**Demand 4/5 · Buildability 4/5**

### 10. Flipkart Seller Hub (Flipkart Marketplace Seller API): catalog listing, inventory/price sync, returns and settlements `closed`

**Why it's powerful:** A top-2 Indian marketplace with a large seller base, deep category schemas, FBF inbound, SPF claims, returns handling and settlement reports. The seller API covers listings v3, orders, shipments, labels, returns, inventory and price.

**Why people bounce off it:** Listings need category-specific Excel templates with 40-80 mandatory columns, HSN/GST and image rules; rejections come back as cryptic error rows. Bulk price changes are sheet uploads. Settlement reports are huge flat files that don't tie to orders, and returns and SPF claims are manual form-filling.

**AI operator:** A catalog and finance copilot: listing authoring from a spec or existing row mapped to the category schema, rejection-fixer, settlement reconciliation that explains every deduction per order and flags overcharges, SPF/return-claim drafter, and bulk price/stock rules with margin floors.

**Killer example:** User: 'Last month's payout was Rs 38k lower than my sales suggest. Find out why and list orders I can contest.' AI reconciles settlement and returns reports order by order, finds 17 orders with reverse shipping charged on a seller-fault-free cancellation, 9 with wrong weight slabs and 4 returns with a different item received, drafts dispute tickets with evidence, and totals the recoverable amount.

**Integration path:** Flipkart Seller API (OAuth2) for listings v3, orders, returns, settlement reports; per-vertical attribute schemas. Thin MCP server, read-heavy MVP (settlement reconciliation, rejection-fixing) first, listing writes second. Risks: API access approval and rate limits; ads/FBF/SPF may need browser fallback.

**Demand 5/5 · Buildability 3/5**

### 11. Ghost (open-source publishing, membership and newsletter platform, MIT, self-hostable or Ghost(Pro)) `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** A fast Node.js CMS bundling posts, paid/free memberships with Stripe, tiers, offers, newsletters, segmented sends, webhooks, custom routing and Handlebars themes, with an Admin API covering almost everything.

**Why people bounce off it:** Segmentation uses NQL filters only partly exposed in the UI; custom URLs need hand-edited routes.yaml and redirects.json; theme changes mean editing Handlebars and re-uploading zips; Substack/WordPress migrations are CSV/JSON wrangling; tier/Stripe/offer pairing is easy to get wrong.

**AI operator:** A publisher-ops copilot: plain-language audiences to NQL filters with count preview and scheduled sends, routes/redirects authoring with validation, theme edits validated by GScan and staged, migration from Substack/WordPress/Mailchimp, offer and tier setup with Stripe consistency checks, bulk SEO cleanup.

**Killer example:** User: 'Email Friday's post only to free members who opened at least 5 of my last 10 newsletters, with a 20% annual-upgrade offer in the footer, and make /issues/ the URL prefix for newsletter posts.' AI builds the NQL filter ('1,284 members match'), creates the offer, schedules the post with that segment, writes the routes.yaml collection and redirects.json entries for old URLs, validates both, and uploads after confirmation.

**Integration path:** Ghost Admin API (JWT from integration key): posts, members with NQL, tiers, offers, newsletters, theme upload/activate, routes and redirects upload, webhooks. Headless, MCP-friendly, tests against local Docker Ghost; require staged previews and test-member sends because sends are real email.

**Demand 3/5 · Buildability 5/5**

### 12. Lago (open-source usage-based billing; Kill Bill, Orb and Metronome as secondary connectors) `open-source`

**Why it's powerful:** The leading open-source alternative to Chargebee/Orb/Metronome, self-hosted to keep billing data in-house. Supports event ingestion at volume, invoicing, tax, dunning and webhooks, aimed at AI/API companies whose pricing changes constantly.

**Why people bounce off it:** Usage pricing is easy to describe and painful to model: billable metrics with aggregation types and filters, plans with standard/graduated/volume/package/percentage charges, pay-in-advance, minimum commitments, wallets, coupons. Setups are subtly wrong and finance only finds out when invoices are off.

**AI operator:** A pricing-ops operator that converts a pricing page or memo into exact metrics, plans, charges and add-ons, replays last month's real usage against the proposed plan to show per-customer invoice deltas before go-live, and handles grandfathering, credits, corrections and invoice-line explanations.

**Killer example:** User: 'From Jan 1 charge 0.40 per 1k tokens for the first 5M tokens a month, then 0.25 above that, plus a 500/month platform fee with 2M tokens included; enterprise stays on the old per-seat plan. What would December have cost each customer?' AI builds the metric and graduated charge, tags enterprise accounts to the legacy plan, replays December in a sandbox, returns old vs new totals per customer, and highlights the 6 accounts whose bill rises over 40%.

**Integration path:** Complete REST API with OpenAPI spec and SDKs; core is open source on Docker Compose; events replay into a sandbox org. MVP: plan builder plus dry-run invoice simulator.

**Demand 3/5 · Buildability 5/5**
